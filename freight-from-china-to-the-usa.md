### **Decoding Essential Shipping Documents**

Proper documentation is the lifeblood of international freight. Missing or inaccurate paperwork is the single most common cause of customs delays and fines. While your freight forwarder will manage this, it’s crucial to know what’s required.

- **Commercial Invoice:** Details the transaction between the seller (your supplier) and the buyer (you). It includes a description of the goods, their value, and the terms of sale.
- **Packing List:** An itemized list of what is inside each box or pallet, including weights and dimensions. It’s used by customs to verify the shipment’s contents.
- **Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB):** This is the contract of carriage. It acts as a receipt for the cargo and a title document. Without it, your goods cannot be released at the destination.
- **Importer Security Filing (ISF 10+2):** For ocean freight, this document *must* be filed with U.S. Customs at least 24 hours before your cargo is loaded onto the vessel in China. Failure to file on time results in a $5,000 penalty. ### **Navigating US Customs Clearance**

Once your freight arrives at a U.S. port or airport, it enters the legal control of U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP). Nothing can leave the port until it is officially “cleared.” The process involves:

1. **Arrival and Entry Filing:** Your customs broker submits the required entry documents to CBP.
2. **Inspection:** CBP may select your shipment for a physical exam or an x-ray to verify its contents and compliance.
3. **Duty and Tax Payment:** Your broker calculates and facilitates the payment of any import duties, taxes, and fees owed to the government.
4. **Release:** Once duties are paid and any inspections are complete, CBP releases the cargo.

This process is intricate. A mistake can lead to your cargo being held for weeks. Our dedicated[ Customs Brokerage](https://aeblogistics.com/customs-brokerage/) service is designed to make this stage seamless and stress-free.

### **Incoterms: The Language of Global Trade**

Incoterms are a set of universal rules that define the responsibilities of the seller and buyer. They clarify who pays for what and who bears the risk at each stage of the shipping journey. The two most common Incoterms for U.S. importers are:

- **EXW (Ex Works):** You, the buyer, are responsible for *everything*. This includes picking up the cargo from your supplier’s factory, arranging export clearance in China, and handling the entire shipping process to your door.
- **FOB (Free On Board):** Your supplier is responsible for all costs and risks until the goods are loaded *on board* the vessel at the origin port in China. From that point forward, you are responsible. For most new importers, FOB is the recommended starting point as it simplifies the origin logistics.

### **What is the difference between freight and cargo?**

While often used interchangeably, there is a subtle difference. **Cargo** refers to the goods themselves being transported (e.g., your shipment of electronics). **Freight** refers to the service or process of transporting that cargo, including the payment for that service. You ship cargo via a freight service.

### **How do I calculate customs duty for cargo from China to USA?**

Customs duty is calculated based on the product’s “HS Code” (a universal classification code), its declared value, and its country of origin. The duty rate is a percentage of the value. For example, if your goods are valued at $10,000 and the duty rate for their HS code is 3%, you would owe $300 in duties.
