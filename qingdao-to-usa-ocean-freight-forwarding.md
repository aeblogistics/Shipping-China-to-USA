### How Do FCL, LCL, Cargo Volume, and Destination Affect the Price?

When arranging freight forwarding from China to the USA, cargo volume determines the pricing model. Shipments above roughly 15 CBM generally cost less per unit under FCL. Smaller loads between 1 and 14 CBM are usually cheaper via LCL, where pricing is billed by volume or chargeable weight based on cargo density.

Destination matters just as much. A 40-foot container landing at Los Angeles or Long Beach typically costs less and moves faster than the same box routed to New York or Savannah. East Coast moves often add Panama Canal transit time and higher base freight costs.

### Which Charges Depend on Incoterms, Customs, and Final Delivery?

Incoterms determine who pays for each leg of the journey. Under FOB, the buyer typically arranges and pays for ocean freight and destination charges, while EXW also makes the buyer responsible for origin pickup costs. Buyers can also request DDP shipping, where the forwarder manages import duties and taxes upfront for a seamless landed cost.

A shipment quote can include customs brokerage, which usually costs around USD 125, depending on how many commodities need clearance. Final delivery charges, such as drayage from the port or liftgate service at a location without a loading dock (an additional USD 100 where needed), are added once the destination address and access details are confirmed.

## Which Ocean Freight Service Fits Your Shipment?

**Cargo volume is the main factor when choosing an ocean freight service: full container loads suit shipments above roughly 15 CBM, while less than container load shipping fits smaller orders that don’t need a dedicated box.** Both options move through Qingdao Port on the same general trade lanes, but they differ in cost structure, handling, and transit time.

### FCL Shipping for Dedicated 20-Foot, 40-Foot, or High-Cube Containers

Full container load (FCL) shipping gives a shipment exclusive use of a 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container. AEB Logistics coordinates FCL bookings, keeping cargo sealed and untouched from loading in Qingdao until it reaches the destination warehouse.

FCL tends to suit larger orders, fragile cargo that benefits from less handling, or businesses looking for a lower cost per unit once volume passes the 15 CBM threshold. Dedicated containers also simplify customs clearance because the entry covers one shipper’s goods, rather than several consignees sharing a box.

### LCL Shipping and Consolidation for Smaller Supplier Orders

LCL shipping combines smaller cargo loads from multiple shippers through freight consolidation, so a business pays only for the space its goods occupy. AEB Logistics manages this consolidated shipping model for customers running smaller test orders, restocking between larger FCL shipments, or combining cargo from several Qingdao-area suppliers into one booking.

The tradeoff is extra time. LCL cargo needs consolidation at a container freight station in China and deconsolidation at a facility in the United States. That adds several days to the overall transit compared with a direct FCL move.

### US Port, Inland Delivery, and Transit-Time Considerations

Port choice changes the door-to-door timeline more than almost any other factor on this route. Vessels from Qingdao to West Coast ports like Los Angeles and Long Beach generally take less time than sailings to East Coast ports such as New York or Savannah, which often route through the Panama Canal or involve transshipment.

Vessel schedules, carrier capacity, and seasonal congestion all affect the final delivery date. After a container clears the discharge port, intermodal shipping by rail or truck carries it inland to major distribution points, such as rail ramps near ORD. While sea transport handles large commercial volumes economically, time-sensitive cargo can also move via air freight or express courier when faster transit is required.

### Factory Pickup, Export Documentation, and Carrier Booking

The process starts with factory pickup in Qingdao. A truck collects cargo from the supplier and takes it to the port or container freight station. AEB Logistics coordinates this leg and prepares the export paperwork needed to get cargo onto a vessel.

That paperwork includes the commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading, along with the correct HS code for each product. Getting the HS code right early matters because it determines the duty rate US Customs and Border Protection applies at entry. Errors here often cause clearance delays.

### US Import Clearance and Required Shipping Documents

US import clearance requires an Importer Security Filing, with ISF filing completed at least 24 hours before the vessel loads in Qingdao, as well as standard entry documents reviewed by US Customs and Border Protection. AEB Logistics coordinates this process and can arrange customs bond options, including an AEB bond for customs entry in applicable cases.

A Power of Attorney is required when a customer asks AEB Logistics to perform customs clearance on their behalf. Importers remain responsible for the duties and taxes assessed against their cargo, regardless of who files the entry.
