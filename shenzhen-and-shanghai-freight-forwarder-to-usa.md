### Air Freight for Urgent and Higher-Value Cargo

 Air freight moves cargo faster than ocean shipping when a launch date or restock deadline is tight. An air shipment from Hong Kong to Los Angeles can take around five days, while general industry guidance places standard air freight from China closer to 7 to 10 days, depending on the destination.

 Air freight suits smaller, higher-value, or time-sensitive shipments, including new product launches and urgent Amazon FBA restocks. It costs more per kilogram than ocean freight, so it works best when speed matters more than the savings from a slower method.

### Ocean Freight With FCL and LCL Options

 Ocean freight from Shenzhen and Shanghai carries most of the volume moving to the USA. Sea freight comes in two main forms: Full Container Load (FCL) and Less Than Container Load (LCL).

 FCL gives you a dedicated 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container, sealed from pickup through delivery. It has fewer handling touchpoints and a lower per-unit cost for larger volumes.

 LCL combines your cargo with goods from other shippers in a shared container, so you pay only for the space your goods use. Ocean freight generally takes at least three weeks, considerably longer than air, but it remains the most economical choice for bulk or heavy cargo.

### Express Courier for Samples and Small Shipments

 Express courier service handles product samples, prototypes, and small urgent parcels that don’t justify a full air or ocean booking. These shipments move as a bundled door-to-door package, with customs handling included in the same service.

 Express pricing runs higher per kilogram than any other method, so it fits small parcels better than inventory-scale restocks.

## How Much Does Shipping From China to the USA Cost?

 Shipping costs from Shenzhen or Shanghai to the USA depend on cargo weight, volume, shipping method, and whether duties come bundled into your quote or get billed separately. There’s no flat rate for every shipment; a 20-foot FCL container, an LCL pallet, and an air shipment all price differently based on chargeable weight and routing.

### What a Transparent Freight Quote Includes

 A complete quote separates every charge instead of folding everything into one number. It includes ocean or air freight, origin handling in China, destination charges in the USA, customs brokerage, and accessorial fees such as liftgate service.

 We can include customs brokerage in your quote. It usually runs around USD 125, depending on the number of commodities in your shipment.

 Ask for an itemized breakdown before approving anything, so you know exactly what’s covered.

### How Cargo Size, Weight, and Routing Affect Shipping Rates

 Freight rates use chargeable weight, which means the higher figure between actual weight and volumetric weight. Ocean freight prices by cubic meters (CBM), while air freight compares gross weight with dimensional weight and charges the higher amount.

 Routing also matters. West Coast ports typically clear cargo faster than East Coast ports for shipments leaving Shenzhen and Shanghai, which can change your total transit time and cost.

### Plan Your Landed Cost Before Booking

 Landed cost includes more than freight charges. It covers declared value, import duties, customs duties, fuel surcharges, and terminal handling charges (THC) at both origin and destination.

 Cargo insurance adds financial protection against transit risk and affects your total cost planning. DDP shipping bundles duties and taxes into one upfront price, while DDU (or DAP) leaves you responsible for paying duties once the goods arrive.

### Potential Charges to Confirm Upfront

 Some charges apply only in specific situations, so confirm them before your cargo ships. These include detention fees if a container sits past its free time, liftgate fees for docks without loading access, and accessorial charges for residential deliveries.

 A forwarder who lists these possibilities in writing before booking is showing real pricing transparency, not offering a bundled number that hides gaps.

## Customs and Amazon FBA Delivery Support

 Customs clearance and Amazon FBA delivery both depend on accurate documentation submitted before your cargo leaves China. We support both within the same shipment process, so sellers don’t have to coordinate a separate customs contact and FBA prep vendor.

### HS Codes, Declared Value, and Customs Compliance

 The HS code on your commercial invoice determines your duty rate and drives customs compliance from the start. We provide HS/HTS code guidance and documentation support, along with import duty and tax estimation support when you provide the shipment details.

 Correct classification before cargo ships helps avoid delays and reclassification once goods reach a U.S. port.

### Importer of Record, Duties, and Customs Bond Options

 Importers remain legally responsible for duties and taxes regardless of the Incoterm used. For U.S. imports, we can arrange an annual customs bond or offer use of an AEB bond on a case-by-case basis.

 We also require a Power of Attorney whenever we’re asked to handle customs clearance on your behalf. For Amazon FBA shipments specifically, Amazon doesn’t act as Importer of Record and won’t accept cargo arriving with unpaid duties, taxes, or shipping charges.

### FBA Labeling, Palletization, and Appointment Coordination

 FBA shipments need labeling, sometimes UPS labeling, product inspection support, and palletization before delivery. We schedule delivery appointments through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal.

 For LCL shipments, we schedule appointments after the goods arrive at our destination-country warehouse. Sellers using their own carrier can still get appointment coordination from us when they provide the bill of lading, Amazon FBA booking form, and carrier manual.

### Inventory Splitting and Delivery to Amazon Fulfillment Centers

 Shipment splitting across multiple Amazon warehouses is common for larger FBA orders. We manage inventory splitting and final-mile delivery by LTL or small parcel service, with warehousing support available before goods move into Amazon’s network.
