## The Ultimate Shipping Checklist: A Step-by-Step Guide

This checklist breaks down the entire process into manageable steps. Follow along to ensure nothing is missed.

### Step 1: Gather Your Shipping Information

Before you can request a quote, you need precise details about your shipment. Inaccurate information can lead to incorrect pricing and unexpected charges later.

- Product Details: What are you shipping? Provide the HS code if you know it. Is the cargo hazardous, fragile, or temperature-sensitive?
- Weight and Dimensions: You’ll need the gross weight, net weight, and dimensions (Length x Width x Height) of each carton, as well as the total volume of the shipment.
- Origin and Destination: Provide the full supplier address in China and the final delivery address in the USA. If it’s going to a warehouse, specify that.
- Incoterms: Clarify the Incoterms agreed upon with your supplier (e.g., EXW, FOB). This determines who is responsible for costs at each stage. For a detailed breakdown of what’s needed, see our guide on[ requesting a quote via the AEB Dashboard](https://support.aeblogistics.com/article/aeb-dashboard-quote-request).

### Step 2: Choose the Right Shipping Method

The best method for international shipping from China to USA depends on your priorities: speed, cost, and volume.

- Air Freight: This is your fastest option, ideal for high-value, low-volume, or time-sensitive goods. Our dedicated[ Air Freight](https://aeblogistics.com/air/) service typically delivers cargo from China to the US in 5-10 days.
- Ocean Freight: The most cost-effective solution for larger shipments. You have two primary choices: 
    - FCL (Full Container Load): You book an entire container for your exclusive use. This is best for high-volume shipments and our[ FCL service](https://aeblogistics.com/fcl/) offers security and efficiency.
    - LCL (Less than Container Load): You share container space with other shippers. Our[ LCL service](https://aeblogistics.com/lcl/) is perfect for smaller shipments that don’t require a full container, offering a great balance of cost and flexibility.
- Express Shipping: Handled by couriers like DHL/FedEx, this is the quickest (3-5 days) but most expensive option, suitable for small parcels and documents.

### Step 4: Prepare Your Documentation Flawlessly

Proper documentation is non-negotiable for a smooth customs process. Missing or incorrect paperwork is the number one cause of delays and fines when shipping freight from China to US.

- Commercial Invoice & Packing List: The foundational documents detailing what you’re shipping and its value.
- Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB): The contract of carriage between you and the shipping line or airline.
- Certificate of Origin: Verifies the country of manufacture, which can affect duty rates.
- Importer Security Filing (ISF 10+2): This must be filed with US Customs at least 24 hours *before* the cargo is loaded onto a vessel destined for the US.

## Answering Key Questions (People Also Ask Section)

### What is the cheapest way for Shipping from China to the USA?

The absolute cheapest method for shipping China to USA is almost always by ocean freight, specifically LCL for smaller loads and FCL for larger ones. Ocean freight allows you to move large volumes at a much lower cost per unit compared to air, though it requires a longer transit time.

### How long does it take for a container Shipping from China to the USA?

Port-to-port transit time for a container ship typically ranges from 20 to 40 days, depending on the specific ports of origin and destination, shipping line schedule, and weather conditions. This does not include time for ground transport, customs clearance, or potential port congestion.

### Do I need a customs broker for Shipping from China to the USA?

For commercial shipments valued over $2,500, you are legally required to use a licensed customs broker to clear your goods through U.S. Customs. A broker ensures all paperwork is filed correctly, duties are paid, and your shipment complies with all regulations, saving you from significant penalties and delays.
