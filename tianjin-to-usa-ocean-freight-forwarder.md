## Ocean Shipping Options from Tianjin

Tianjin offers two practical ocean freight options to the US: FCL for larger volumes and LCL for smaller or mixed-supplier loads. Both move through the Port of Tianjin, often called Tianjin Xingang Port or simply Xingang Port. As a premier maritime hub in northern China, Tianjin Port processes extensive container traffic bound for American terminals.

The right choice depends on volume, timing, and how many suppliers are contributing to a shipment.

### When Does FCL Make Sense?

Full Container Load shipping makes sense when a shipment fills a meaningful share of a container, generally more than 10 to 15 cubic meters. Cargo travels in its own dedicated 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container, without sharing or splitting along the way.

For heavier, bulkier, or higher-volume cargo, FCL usually offers the lowest per-unit cost. It also limits handling because workers don’t open and repack the container at a consolidation point. That can matter for fragile goods or cargo that needs tighter inventory control.

### When Is LCL or Multi-Supplier Consolidation a Better Fit?

LCL suits shipments too small to justify a full container, typically under 10 cubic meters. At origin, cargo gets combined with other shippers’ goods in a shared container, then separated at the destination.

This works well for businesses sourcing from several Tianjin-area suppliers at once. Multi-supplier consolidation combines several factory orders into one container booking, simplifying documentation and reducing the number of separate shipments an importer needs to track. Amazon sellers restocking with smaller, frequent orders often find LCL or consolidated shipping a better fit for their cash flow and inventory pace than committing to a full container.

### How Do Routes and Transit Times Affect Your Plan?

Transit times from Tianjin depend heavily on the destination port and route. Shipments to US West Coast ports generally move faster than those going to the East Coast, where service often involves longer ocean legs or transshipment.

Door-to-door ocean freight from China to the USA, including Tianjin-origin cargo, generally takes 30 to 40 days according to our published estimates. That includes origin trucking, ocean transit, customs processing, and final delivery. Port congestion, customs holds, weather, and peak-season demand can extend that window, so we build in a buffer rather than promise a fixed delivery date.

Specialized cargo, such as oversized or irregularly shaped goods, may need open-top containers instead of standard dry containers. Flagging this early is worthwhile because it affects both routing and cost.

When time-sensitive cargo cannot wait for ocean transit, air freight offers a faster shipping alternative. Air freight from Tianjin typically departs via Tianjin Binhai International Airport, connecting to central US gateways such as ORD before final transport. For small parcels or urgent product samples, an express courier provides door-to-door expedited delivery.

### What Determines FCL and LCL Freight Rates?

FCL freight cost is quoted as a flat rate per container, regardless of how full it is. The price stays the same whether the container is packed tight or only three-quarters full. LCL freight cost is calculated per cubic meter, so the price scales with the space the cargo occupies.

Carrier capacity, fuel surcharges, and seasonal demand affect rates for both options. Shipping during peak periods, such as the weeks before major US retail seasons, tends to push rates higher across the board.

### How Do Duties, Insurance, and Delivery Affect Landed Cost?

Landed cost is the total cost of a shipment once it reaches its final US destination, including freight, duties, taxes, insurance, and delivery. Incoterms like FOB or EXW determine where cost and risk transfer from supplier to importer. That directly shapes what appears in a freight quote and what the importer pays separately.

Cargo insurance protects against loss or damage in transit, so it’s worth including in the total cost rather than treating it as an afterthought. Importers who want duties and fees included in one upfront price can use DDP shipping (Delivered Duty Paid), which combines freight, customs, and delivery in a single price.

For low-value e-commerce orders, shipments may qualify for the Section 321 de minimis exemption, allowing duty-free import into the United States. Regardless of clearance type, the importer of record remains responsible for the accuracy of all underlying customs information.

## How Does Door-to-Door Shipping and Customs Clearance Work?

Door-to-door shipping from Tianjin moves cargo from the factory floor to a US address in one coordinated process. Along the way, the shipment goes through customs clearance at both the export and import stages. Breaking the process into stages makes it easier to see where delays typically happen and what documentation can help prevent them.

### From Factory Pickup to Export Departure

The process starts with factory pickup at the Tianjin-area supplier, followed by trucking to the port and export customs clearance in China. Export customs clearance confirms the cargo can legally leave China and matches its declared value and classification.

Once customs clears the cargo, it loads onto the vessel for ocean transit. At this stage, officials also check any required export license, and the shipment officially begins its journey to the US.

### Which Documents and US Import Filings Are Needed?

US customs clearance requires a specific set of documents, and missing or inconsistent paperwork is the most common cause of port delays. Complete shipping documentation must be prepared accurately before departure. Core shipping documents include the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin, with product descriptions, quantities, and values matching exactly across them.

Accurate HS codes and HTS code classification, based on the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, determine the duty rate. They must reflect the actual product, not just what the supplier suggests. For ocean cargo, importers must submit an ISF filing before the vessel loads, giving US Customs and Border Protection advance shipment data.

A customs broker typically handles tasks like entry filing and duty calculation, and may require a customs bond depending on shipment value. AEB Logistics includes customs brokerage coordination in a shipment quote, which usually costs around USD 125 depending on the number of commodities. ### From US Arrival to Warehouse or Amazon FBA Delivery

Once cargo clears US customs, it moves to last-mile delivery, either directly to a business address, a warehousing and distribution facility, or an Amazon FBA fulfillment center. If workers haven’t completed palletization and labeling at origin, they often do it at this stage.

For Amazon-bound cargo, we schedule delivery appointments, and the FBA plan may require us to split inventory across multiple fulfillment centers. Sellers who want to hold inventory outside Amazon can use bonded warehousing or conventional storage to manage inventory flow and replenish fulfillment centers on their own schedule.
