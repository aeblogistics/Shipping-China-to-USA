Shipping products from China to the USA for the first time can feel overwhelming. You pick a [freight forwarder](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-from-china-to-the-usa/), agree on a quote, and assume the price covers everything.

Then extra charges show up for warehousing, [customs](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarders-china-to-usa-shipping-with-full-customs-support/), or Amazon FBA prep work you didn’t know you needed. Freight forwarder [pricing](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-from-china-to-usa/) often includes more than the base shipping rate, and missing these extra costs can throw off your budget before your goods even arrive.

If you plan to sell through Amazon, you also need a forwarder who understands FBA prep rules and labeling requirements. A forwarder who communicates clearly and explains fees upfront can save you money and stress.

This article breaks down the costs that catch first-time importers off guard. You’ll learn what to ask about pricing, warehousing, communication, and FBA prep before you sign a contract with a freight forwarder.

## Origin terminal handling charges

Before your goods even leave China, the port terminal charges a fee to move your container. This is called the origin terminal handling charge, or origin THC.

It covers lifting your container on or off a truck, moving it around the yard, and loading it onto the ship. You won’t always see this fee listed clearly in your first quote.

Some [freight forwarders](https://gofreight.com/glossary/terminal-handling-charges-thc) bundle it into the [ocean freight rate](https://aeblogistics.com/container-shipping-rates-china-to-usa/). Others list it as a separate line item.

The terminal sets this price, not the shipping line. The [charge can vary by port](https://holocargo.com/blog/what-is-thc-terminal-handling-charge), even if you ship the same size container on the same route.

Who pays this fee depends on your Incoterms agreement with your supplier. Under FOB terms, your supplier usually pays the origin THC.

Under EXW terms, you may end up paying it yourself, even though it happens on the Chinese side of the [shipment](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china/). Ask your forwarder to show origin THC as its own line item.

This makes it easier to compare quotes and spot [hidden markups](https://aeblogistics.com/container-shipping-costs-china-to-usa/) before you commit to a shipment.

## Export customs clearance fees

When you ship from China, your [goods need to clear Chinese customs](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-china-to-usa/) before they [leave the country](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-the-usa/). This step comes with its own set of charges, separate from anything you pay in the US.

Your freight forwarder or a [customs agent in China](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-us/) usually handles this process for you. They review your paperwork, confirm your goods match the declared value, and file the export entry.

Export clearance fees are often smaller than import fees, but they still add up. You may see charges for document preparation, customs filing, and inspection if your shipment gets flagged for review.

Some forwarders bundle this fee into your overall quote, while others list it separately. Ask for a clear breakdown so you know exactly what you’re paying for before your goods leave China.

Missing or incorrect paperwork can delay your shipment and add extra costs. Your [freight forwarder fees](https://www.exfreight.com/guide-to-international-air-freight-rates-and-freight-forwarder-fees/) should reflect the full scope of export handling, not just the ocean or air transport cost.

## Documentation and bill of lading fees

Paperwork fees catch a lot of first-time importers off guard. Every shipment needs documents to move legally across borders, and freight forwarders charge for preparing and handling them.

The bill of lading is one of the most important. It works as three things at once: proof the carrier has your goods, a contract for the shipment, and the document that lets you (or your buyer) claim the cargo at the port.

You can read more about how this document functions in this [guide to bill of lading basics](https://virginia-gtfs.com/freight-importing-101-customs-clearance-fees-and-paperwork). Expect a separate documentation fee on top of your main freight charge.

This covers the forwarder’s time preparing export and import paperwork, commercial invoices, and packing lists. Rates vary by forwarder and shipment size.

[Ask your forwarder](https://aeblogistics.com/china-usa-freight-forwarding-8-questions-to-ask-before-choosing-a-provider/) for a [full breakdown](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-10-capabilities-us-importers-should-evaluate/) before you book. Some quotes bundle documentation fees into the base rate, while others list them as add-ons.

[Comparing forwarders](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-9-quote-checks-before-you-ship/) side by side helps you spot which one is [charging fairly for paperwork handling](https://www.freightos.com/freight-resources/freight-forwarder-calculating-costs-and-fees/). If you’re new to importing, budget for these fees separately from ocean or air freight costs.

They’re small individually but add up across [multiple shipments](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-for-smbs/).

## Container pickup and drayage charges

Once your container lands at a US port, someone has to move it to a [warehouse](https://aeblogistics.com/warehousing/) or fulfillment center. This part of the shipping process is called [drayage](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-shipping-from-china-to-usa/), and it’s a cost many first-time importers forget to ask about.

[Drayage fees](https://warehousingcosts.com/guides/port-drayage-costs) typically include a base linehaul rate, plus extra charges for chassis rental, fuel, and wait time at the port. These add-ons can raise your total cost by hundreds of dollars per container.

Chassis fees cover the wheeled frame that carries the container from the port to its next stop. [Chassis and trucking surcharges](https://freightsurcharge.com/blog/chassis-fees-container-trucking-surcharges-us-imports-2026) can add $500 to $2,000 per container, depending on your port and carrier.

Ask your freight forwarder for a [full drayage quote](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-quote/) before your shipment arrives. Find out if the quote includes chassis fees, fuel surcharges, and terminal wait charges, or if these will be billed separately.

Also ask how [drayage pricing](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-the-usa-2/) changes if your container gets delayed at the port. Delays can lead to per diem charges, which are daily fees for keeping a container past its free time.

## Cargo consolidation or deconsolidation fees

If you’re shipping less than a full container, your goods get grouped with cargo from other shippers. This is called consolidation.

It lets you avoid paying for space you don’t need. At the destination, your freight forwarder has to separate your goods from everyone else’s.

This step is called [deconsolidation](https://aeblogistics.com/china-freight-forwarder/), and it happens at a container freight station, or CFS. Both steps come with fees that are easy to miss when you’re comparing quotes.

Origin consolidation typically runs [$12 to $40 per cubic meter](https://warehousingcosts.com/guides/cfs-charges). [Destination CFS handling](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-shanghai-to-usa/) at US ports can cost [$40 to $95 per cubic meter](https://warehousingcosts.com/guides/cfs-charges).

Deconsolidation is usually billed as a flat fee on top of that. Ask your forwarder to break out these charges separately in your quote.

Some forwarders combine them into a single line item, which makes it harder to [compare offers](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-services-comparing-china-usa-providers/). If your order volume grows, you may reach a point where booking a full container costs less than paying [LCL consolidation](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-china-to-usa-2/) and deconsolidation fees.

It’s worth asking your forwarder to run the numbers both ways.

## Customs broker entry fees

Many first-time importers forget that clearing customs costs money beyond duties and taxes. A [customs broker](https://aeblogistics.com/customs-brokerage/) charges an entry fee for filing paperwork with U.S. Customs and Border Protection.

In 2026, [standard entry fees run between $150 and $250](https://suaidglobal.com/insights/customs-broker-fees-2026/), though total costs can climb past $400 depending on your shipment. Your final price depends on a few factors.

Shipments with multiple product lines usually cost more, since each line requires separate classification. Some brokers charge as low as $85 to $125 per clearance, with extra fees for things like an Importer Security Filing or a customs bond.

You’ll also need a bond before your goods can [clear customs](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-from-china-to-usa-essential-customs-strategies/). This bond guarantees that duties and fees get paid, and it’s a separate cost from the broker’s entry fee.

Ask your broker for a full breakdown before you ship, so you know what you’re paying for. [Comparing quotes](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-quote-target-price-benefits-budgeting-comparison-customization-negotiation-satisfactory-solution/) from a few brokers helps you avoid overpaying.

Since fees vary by port, shipment type, and broker, getting multiple quotes gives you a clearer picture of fair market pricing for your specific import.

## Import duties and tariff assessments

Many first-time importers assume they only need to budget for one duty rate. In reality, your shipment may face several layers of tariffs stacked on top of each other.

[Section 301 tariffs](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-from-china-to-the-usa/) apply to many goods made in China, and rates vary by product category. Some shipments also get hit with [Section 232 tariffs](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-us-2/), which target specific materials like steel and aluminum.

On top of that, [Section 122 tariffs](https://firstlinkpartners.com/us-import-tariffs/) can apply depending on current trade policy, adding another cost you need to plan for. Your freight forwarder should help you understand which tariffs apply to your HTS code before you ship.

This step matters because guessing wrong can mean paying much more than expected once your goods reach customs. You also need to know how [customs duty and import tax](https://gofreight.com/blog/customs-duty-import-tax-guide) get calculated, since the dutiable value includes more than just the product price.

Freight, insurance, and certain fees can factor into that number too. Ask for a full breakdown of expected duties before you commit to a forwarder.

A clear quote upfront helps you avoid surprise charges when your shipment clears customs.

## Port security and terminal handling fees

When your goods reach a US port, you’ll face new fees that many first-time importers don’t expect. A [terminal handling charge](https://gofreight.com/glossary/terminal-handling-charges-thc) covers the cost of moving your container on and off the ship at the port.

The terminal operator charges this fee, and your carrier or freight forwarder passes it on to you. Sometimes it shows up as a separate line item, but other times it’s hidden in your overall freight rate.

Ask your forwarder to break down these charges clearly. If you don’t, it’s way too easy to miss what you’re actually paying for.

Port security fees add another layer. These charges pay for screening and safety measures at US ports.

They’re usually smaller than terminal handling fees, but after a few shipments, you’ll notice the total. Port charges can make up a big chunk of your total shipping bill, especially on smaller orders.

When you compare [freight forwarders for Alibaba imports](https://suaidglobal.com/insights/freight-forwarding-cost/), ask each one to list terminal and security fees separately from their [base rate](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-2026-comparison-costs-and-best-services/).

This lets you spot hidden markups and [compare quotes](https://aeblogistics.com/10-factors-in-comparing-china-to-usa-forwarders/) more accurately. Some forwarders bundle these fees to make their rates look lower, so always read quotes carefully before you commit.

## Destination warehouse handling charges

Once your goods land in the USA, they don’t just roll straight to Amazon or your store. Most freight forwarders route your shipment through a destination warehouse first, which means more fees.

You’ll usually get billed for receiving, unloading, and sorting your cargo. Some [warehouse invoices include 11 or more line items](https://freightsurcharge.com/blog/warehouse-receiving-handling-fees-importer-guide-2026) for things that sound like basic storage, but each gets billed separately.

If you’re prepping for [Amazon FBA](https://aeblogistics.com/fba-fulfillment-by-amazon/), expect extra charges. These can include [labeling](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-to-amazon-fba/), palletizing, and repacking cartons for Amazon’s requirements.

Each of these tasks adds a fee on top of standard handling. Storage time matters too.

Most warehouses give you a few free days before daily storage fees start. If your forwarder is slow to arrange pickup or delivery, those charges pile up fast.

Before you sign with a forwarder, ask for a full breakdown of destination handling costs. Compare how each charges for receiving, prep work, and storage so you don’t get hit with [surprise fees](https://aeblogistics.com/6-common-mistakes-when-shipping-from-china-to-usa/) at the end.

## How To Compare Quotes, Warehousing, And Service Scope

Freight forwarder quotes can differ by hundreds of dollars for the same shipment. They include different fees, service levels, and assumptions.

Check what each quote actually covers. Look at how storage terms affect your Amazon FBA prep schedule, and how easy it is to reach your forwarder if something goes wrong.

### Comparing Like-For-Like Freight Forwarding Quotes

Two quotes rarely cover the same services, even when the headline price looks similar. One forwarder might include customs clearance and destination drayage, while another just quotes ocean freight and leaves the rest as extra charges.

Before you compare numbers, [normalize the scope of each quote](https://cargolinked.com/guides/best-way-to-compare-freight-quotes) so you’re comparing the same services. Check these items on every quote:

- **[Incoterm used](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarding-port-to-port-ddp-and-fba/)** (FOB, CIF, DDP, etc.)
- **Origin and destination handling fees**
- **Customs brokerage and duty payment**
- **Fuel and peak season surcharges**
- **Free time before storage or detention fees start**
 
Ask each forwarder to confirm what’s included and what’s billed separately. This is especially important for [Alibaba imports](https://asp-cargo.com/how-to-compare-freight-rates-scope-surcharges-and-quote-assumptions/), since suppliers usually quote FOB prices and leave you responsible for the rest.

### Warehousing Terms, Storage Windows, And Consolidation Needs

Warehousing terms shape how much flexibility you have after your goods land in the US. Ask each forwarder how many free storage days they give you, and what they charge per pallet or per day after that.

If you’re prepping goods for Amazon FBA, find out if the forwarder offers labeling, box prep, and repackaging on-site. Some charge per unit for FBA prep, others bundle it into a flat warehousing fee.

Consolidation matters if you order from multiple suppliers. Ask if the forwarder can combine shipments from different factories into one container before shipping, since this can lower your per-unit freight cost.

Also, confirm how long they’ll hold goods while waiting for all your supplier shipments to arrive. It’s one of those details that can save you a headache later.

### Communication Standards And Shipment Visibility

You need to know how a [forwarder handles updates](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarders-china-to-usa-global-vs-niche-providers/) before you sign a contract. Ask what [tracking tools](https://aeblogistics.com/top-qualities-of-leading-forwarders/) they offer and how often they send status updates.

Some forwarders give you a dashboard with [real-time container tracking](https://aeblogistics.com/can-i-track-my-shipment/). Others just send emails from an account manager.

Find out who you’ll contact if there’s a delay, damage, or [customs hold](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarders-from-china-to-usa-top-10-specialists-for-complex-customs-clearance/). Confirm their response time during your business hours.

This [20-point evaluation checklist](https://content.ballastmarkets.com/learn/modules/freight-forwarder-selection-checklist/) covers questions to ask about communication before you pick a forwarder. Poor visibility during transit can really mess with your FBA shipment planning and inventory timing.

## Planning for Amazon FBA And Import Compliance

Selling on Amazon FBA adds another layer of rules on top of standard customs work. You’ll need to plan for prep and labeling, get your import paperwork right, and choose a forwarder who can handle your specific shipment.

### Amazon FBA Prep, Labeling, And Appointment Requirements

Amazon has strict rules for how your products arrive at its fulfillment centers. Every unit needs the right FNSKU barcode, and cartons must have the correct shipment ID before leaving the forwarder’s warehouse.

You also have to book a delivery appointment for larger shipments. If your freight shows up without one, or without the right labels, Amazon can reject it or cause a delay.

Some forwarders offer [prep services like labeling and carton rework at their own warehouse](https://www.shipit.com/post/freight-forwarder-amazon-fba-prep-labeling-and-delivery-options) before sending goods to Amazon. This is handy if your supplier can’t apply FBA labels correctly.

Ask your forwarder if they check pallet dimensions and weight limits too. Amazon will reject shipments that don’t meet its guidelines, and that’s a hassle you don’t want.

### Customs Documentation, Duties, And Importer Responsibilities

As the importer, you’re legally responsible for your shipment’s customs paperwork, even if your forwarder handles the filing. This includes your commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading.

You also need the correct HTS code for each product. This code sets your duty rate, and if you get it wrong, you could face fines or shipment holds.

First-time importers should know how [Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods](https://tariffpro.app/en/blog/amazon-fba-import-compliance-customs-classification) affect their landed cost. These add extra duties on top of standard rates.

You’ll also need an Importer of Record, which can be you or a designated agent. Before you ship, make sure you have:

- Your HTS classification for each SKU
- Any needed FDA, FCC, or other agency approval
- Your total duty rate, including Section 301 surcharges
 
### Choosing a Forwarder Based on Shipment Complexity And Support Needs

Not every shipment needs the same level of support. A single pallet of compliant, pre-labeled goods can usually go straight to Amazon.

If you have a more complex order—maybe it needs relabeling, inspection, or repacking—you’ll want a forwarder that offers [warehouse staging](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-ningbo-to-usa/) first. This gives you a chance to fix issues before Amazon sees them.

When comparing forwarders, ask how they handle exceptions like customs holds, mislabeled cartons, or Amazon appointment rejections. A [good freight forwarder manages your goods from factory to fulfillment center](https://suaidglobal.com/insights/amazon-fba-freight-forwarder/), including customs clearance and [final delivery](https://aeblogistics.com/shanghai-freight-forwarder/).

If you’re new to importing, look for a forwarder that offers clear communication and [hands-on problem solving](https://aeblogistics.com/best-freight-forwarder-china-to-usa/), not just booking and tracking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

First-time importers often focus on the base freight rate and miss the smaller charges that add up fast. Here’s what you need to know about hidden fees, forwarder differences, warehousing costs, FBA prep, quote comparisons, and all-in shipping estimates.

### What fees should first-time importers expect beyond the quoted freight rate?

Your [freight quote](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-quote-china-to-usa/) covers the ocean or air transport, but it rarely includes everything else tied to your shipment. Plan for origin terminal handling charges, which cover moving your cargo through the port before it loads onto the vessel.

Export customs clearance fees are another cost you’ll see. Your goods have to clear customs in China before leaving.

Documentation and bill of lading fees also show up separately. These cover the paperwork that proves ownership and shipping terms for your cargo.

If your forwarder arranges pickup at the factory or warehouse, expect container pickup and drayage charges too. When your shipment is smaller than a full container, you’ll pay cargo consolidation or deconsolidation fees, since your goods get combined with other shipments to fill space.

### How do China-based and U.S.-based forwarders differ in pricing, communication, and service coverage?

China-based forwarders often have lower rates because they work directly with factories and ports, skipping the middleman markup. You might also get faster answers about export documents, since these forwarders handle Chinese customs rules every day and can [explain freight forwarder costs and charges](https://ship4wd.com/logistics-shipping/freight-forwarder-cost) tied to origin processes.

U.S.-based forwarders usually focus more on import-side services, like customs entry filing and delivery to your warehouse or Amazon facility. You’ll probably get communication in your time zone, which matters if you need quick answers during business hours.

Service coverage is different too. A China-based forwarder may not offer strong support once your goods land in the U.S., while a U.S.-based forwarder may charge more for origin services like factory pickup, since [customs brokers and freight forwarders handle different parts of the import process](https://eximpe.com/blog/b2b/freight-forwarder-vs-customs-house-agent).

### Which warehousing and storage charges can increase the total cost of an import shipment?

Storage fees can pile up fast if your cargo sits at the port or warehouse longer than expected. Most ports start charging daily fees once your free storage window ends.

These charges jump the longer your shipment waits for pickup. If you need your forwarder to hold goods before shipping them to Amazon or your own warehouse, you’ll see extra costs.

Some forwarders charge by the pallet, while others use cubic foot rates. It’s smart to ask for exact rates before you pick a storage plan.

If your shipment needs repackaging or labeling in storage, you’ll pay a separate labor fee on top of the basic storage charge.

### What Amazon FBA preparation services should be confirmed before goods leave China?

Before your goods leave the factory, check if your forwarder can handle [FNSKU labeling](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-top-10-services-for-e-commerce-brands/). Amazon wants this on every unit, so missing it isn’t really an option.

See if the forwarder offers poly bagging or bubble wrap services if your product needs extra protection during shipping. Sometimes it’s easy to forget these details, but they matter.

Ask whether the forwarder can split your shipment across multiple Amazon fulfillment centers. Amazon often requires this for larger orders, and it’s a headache if you don’t plan ahead.

You’ll want confirmation on carton labeling requirements too. Wrong labels can stall your shipment at the Amazon warehouse, which nobody wants.

Get pricing for these services in writing before your goods ship. FBA prep fees jump all over the place depending on the forwarder.

### How can importers compare freight quotes accurately when services and surcharges differ?

Request an [itemized quote](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-in-depth-2026-comparison-guide/) from each forwarder instead of a single lump sum. That way, you can see exactly what’s included, like [origin handling, customs clearance, and documentation fees](https://www.finepg.com/blog-understanding-freight-forwarder-fees), and what’s billed separately.

Compare the same shipment details across quotes—container size, weight, delivery address—so you’re not looking at apples and oranges. Ask each forwarder to list any accessorial fees, like fuel surcharges or peak season charges. These can swing the total cost in ways that aren’t always obvious.

Add up the full cost for each option once you have multiple itemized quotes. Don’t just look at the base freight rate—it’s rarely the whole story.

### What documents, customs costs, and delivery arrangements should be included in an all-in shipping estimate?

Your [all-in estimate](https://aeblogistics.com/door-to-door-shipping-quotes-china-to-usa/) really needs to cover the bill of lading, commercial invoice, and packing list. These documents are essential for customs clearance on both sides.

Don’t forget to factor in customs duties and tariffs. They change depending on your product’s classification code, so double-check that detail.

Delivery arrangements can get tricky. Make sure you know if your estimate includes last-mile delivery to your warehouse or Amazon fulfillment center, or if that’s a separate charge after customs clearance.

Honestly, it’s smart to ask your forwarder to walk you through [the full freight forwarding process, from documents to final delivery](https://www.shipit.com/post/freight-forwarding-service-101-process-costs-and-documents). That way, you’ll know exactly what’s in your quote and what’s not.
