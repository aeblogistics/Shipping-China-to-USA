## Which Shipping Method Fits Your Cargo and Deadline?

 Sea freight suits larger, less urgent cargo, while air freight and express courier work well for time-sensitive or smaller shipments. The right choice depends on your budget, cargo volume, and how soon you need the goods in the United States.

### FCL and LCL Sea Freight for Commercial Goods

 Full Container Load (FCL) gives you a dedicated 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container. It works well for shipments large enough to fill most of the space. Less Than Container Load (LCL) shares container space with other cargo, so you pay only for the volume you use. It’s a good fit for smaller or recurring orders.

 AEB Logistics coordinates both FCL and LCL sea freight from Shenzhen, including customs clearance coordination and door-to-door delivery once the cargo reaches the United States. ### Air Freight and Express Courier for Urgent Shipments

 Air freight moves faster than sea freight but costs more per kilogram. It can suit high-value, time-sensitive, or relatively small shipments. General guidance for standard air freight from China puts transit at roughly 8 to 10 days.

 Express courier services take closer to 3 to 5 days for urgent parcels. When time is critical, express shipping from Shenzhen to USA destinations provides the fastest transit window.

 As a general comparison, an air shipment from Hong Kong to Los Angeles can take around five days, while ocean freight on the same route takes at least three weeks. That’s the trade-off: speed versus cost per kilogram.

## How Are Documents and US Customs Clearance Coordinated?

 US customs clearance depends on accurate paperwork prepared before cargo arrives. Preparing complete shipping documents in advance ensures smooth transit. Missing or inconsistent shipping documentation is the most common reason a shipment gets held at the port.

### Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Transport Documents

 The commercial invoice lists cargo value and product details used to assess duties, while the packing list details box counts and weights. A bill of lading for ocean freight or an air waybill for air freight serves as the transport contract.

 This airway bill or ocean bill provides key tracking details for customs entry. Some goods may require a certificate of origin, and specific restricted product categories need an import license.

 Getting these documents right the first time helps avoid a customs hold that could add days to a shipment already in transit.

### HS Code Classification, Customs Bonds, and Importer Responsibilities

 HS code classification determines the duty rate for a shipment. An incorrect code can lead to an incorrect duty bill or a customs review. AEB Logistics provides HS/HTS code guidance and documentation support, as well as import duty and tax estimates when importers provide shipment details ahead of time.

 For US imports, AEB Logistics can arrange an annual US customs bond or offer use of an AEB bond on a case-by-case basis. If an importer asks AEB Logistics to handle customs clearance, they must provide a Power of Attorney.

 Importers remain responsible for duties and taxes. For Amazon FBA shipments, Amazon doesn’t act as Importer of Record and won’t accept cargo with unpaid duties, taxes, or shipping charges.

### Customs Brokerage, Release, and Delivery Updates

 Customs brokerage coordination covers the customs declaration and entry filing needed to release cargo from the port. On request, AEB Logistics can provide an itemized charge breakdown for [customs brokerage](https://aeblogistics.com/customs-brokerage/) services, so importers can see clearance costs alongside freight charges.

 Shipment status updates cover milestones such as Export Customs, Import Entry, Import Arrival, and Delivered. These updates show procurement teams exactly where their cargo is in the clearance process.
