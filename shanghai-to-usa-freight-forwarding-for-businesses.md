## Choose the Right Shipping Service for Your Cargo

**The right shipping method for Shanghai to USA freight depends on your cargo’s urgency, volume, and value per kilogram, not on which service has the lowest quoted rate.** Businesses moving bulk inventory and those replenishing Amazon FBA stock often need different answers.

International freight forwarding from Shanghai offers four main transport options: ocean FCL, ocean LCL, air freight, and express courier. Each balances cost and speed differently.

### Ocean Freight: FCL Containers or LCL Shipping

FCL shipping (Full Container Load) gives one shipper exclusive use of a container, available in 20-foot, 40-foot, and 40-foot high-cube options. It works best for larger volumes of sea freight, since a dedicated, sealed container minimizes handling and lowers the per-unit cost.

LCL (Less than Container Load) lets smaller shipments share container space with other cargo. You pay only for the volume you use, making it a good fit for businesses that don’t yet have enough product to fill a container. For [shipping from Shanghai to the USA with supplier consolidation](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa/shanghai-to-usa-shipping-with-supplier-consolidation/), LCL often makes more financial sense than paying for unused container space.

### Air Freight and Express Courier for Urgent Shipments

Air freight suits shipments where speed matters more than cost per kilogram. As a planning reference, an air shipment from Hong Kong to Los Angeles can take around five days. General Amazon FBA guidance puts standard air freight closer to 7 to 10 days, depending on the shipment and destination. Treat these estimates as planning ranges, not guaranteed schedules, since customs processing and carrier capacity affect actual arrival times.

Express courier services are the fastest option for samples and small, time-critical parcels, bundling pickup, customs, and delivery into one service. They cost more per kilogram than standard air freight, so they’re better suited to urgent small shipments than full inventory restocks.

### Cargo Consolidation Across Multiple Suppliers

Businesses sourcing from more than one Shanghai-area supplier can combine their shipments in one container instead of booking separate freight for each vendor. Multi-supplier consolidation lowers per-unit freight costs and simplifies the paperwork trail: one bill of lading covers the combined cargo instead of several.

## What Will Your Shipment Cost?

**Your total cost depends on chargeable weight, shipping mode, and how much of the move your quote actually covers, not a flat per-container or per-kilogram number.** A 40-foot container, a shared LCL pallet, and an air shipment all have different pricing, so comparing quotes line by line matters more than comparing one headline figure.

### What Determines Freight Rates and Chargeable Weight?

Base shipping rates and carrier invoices depend on chargeable weight, which is whichever is greater: actual weight or volumetric weight. Ocean freight pricing uses cubic meters (CBM) for LCL or a flat container rate for FCL. Air freight and express shipments are priced per chargeable kilogram, so a light but bulky shipment can cost more than its scale weight suggests.

### Freight Charges vs. Total Landed Cost

The quoted freight rate is only one part of your total landed cost. Fuel surcharges, terminal handling charges, destination drayage, and customs brokerage add to the base rate. Cargo insurance also depends on declared value, so higher-value shipments carry higher insurance costs.

We can include customs brokerage in a shipment quote on request. It usually costs around USD 125, depending on the number of commodities in the shipment.

### DDP vs. DAP: Confirm Who Pays Duties and Delivery Charges

DDP shipping (Delivered Duty Paid) means duties and taxes are settled before your cargo arrives, so you won’t face a separate customs bill at delivery. DAP (Delivered at Place) leaves duty payment to the buyer after arrival.

“DDU” (Delivered Duty Unpaid) is older terminology that largely overlaps with DAP under current Incoterms usage, so confirm what your supplier or forwarder means before assuming it covers certain charges.

Confirm the scope of either arrangement in writing before booking. Coverage varies by shipment and provider.

## Customs Support for Compliant US Imports

**US customs clearance for Shanghai-origin cargo depends on accurate paperwork prepared before the goods leave China, not on resolving issues after arrival.** Adhering to established customs procedures prevents unexpected cargo holds at US arrival ports.

A missing customs bond, vague product description, or unconfirmed Importer of Record can delay entry filing even when the freight is moving on schedule.

### Commercial Invoices, Packing Lists, and Transport Documents

Every shipment needs a commercial invoice, a packing list, and a transport document: a bill of lading for ocean freight or an air waybill for air cargo. These documents travel with the shipment and support the import entry filed with Customs and Border Protection. Depending on the product, you may also need a certificate of origin.

Make product descriptions specific. CBP has noted that vague terms like “parts” or “general merchandise” don’t provide enough information to identify and assess cargo.

### HS Code Classification and Applicable Section 301 Tariffs

The HS code on your commercial invoice determines the duty rate applied at entry. Getting it wrong can lead to a costly reclassification later. Certain product categories from China also carry Section 301 tariffs on top of standard duty rates. We offer HS/HTS code guidance and documentation support, but check current Section 301 tariff rates against the Harmonized Tariff Schedule maintained by the US International Trade Commission. Rates and product lists change.

### Importer of Record Responsibilities

Importers remain responsible for duties and taxes, regardless of who files the entry. The Importer of Record is associated with the customs entry and is accountable for accurate classification, value, and origin information, even when a licensed broker prepares and transmits the filing. [Our customs brokerage](https://aeblogistics.com/customs-brokerage/) support works alongside your appointed broker rather than replacing that accountability.

### Customs Bonds, and Power of Attorney

A customs bond financially guarantees that the importer will meet its obligations to CBP, and commercial imports generally need one. Securing a continuous US customs bond is standard practice for regular commercial importers.

For US imports, we can arrange an annual bond or offer use of an AEB bond on a case-by-case basis. You’ll need to provide a Power of Attorney if you ask AEB Logistics to perform customs clearance on your behalf.
