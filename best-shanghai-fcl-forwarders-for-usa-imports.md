## Kuehne+Nagel

Kuehne+Nagel operates close to 1,300 locations across nearly 100 countries, making it a natural choice for importers moving steady volumes across several trade lanes. Its China teams handle export documents, factory pickup, customs brokerage, and ocean and air bookings.

## DHL Global Forwarding

DHL Global Forwarding is separate from DHL’s parcel courier arm and focuses on customized freight. It connects Shanghai and other Chinese export hubs to US ports and airports, and can arrange customs clearance on request.

Rates are quoted per shipment and vary with weight, volume, route, and market conditions. Support covers Chinese and English time zones by phone, email, and portal. Importers who already use DHL for other logistics can bring ocean freight into one account relationship. Regulated or higher-value cargo is also a natural fit.

## DSV

DSV offers air and sea freight, road transport, warehousing, and customs-related services through a global network. For importers sourcing from several countries, using one provider to handle documentation across those lanes can keep paperwork consistent.

Ask for lane-specific pricing, since container type, route, and current capacity all affect the rate. DSV fits businesses with recurring freight that need customs and warehousing coordinated across regions. A one-off Shanghai container may get less attention than a standing program.

## Flexport

Flexport is a digital freight platform for ocean and air shipments from Shanghai and other Chinese ports. You can see pricing before booking and follow tracking updates from pickup through delivery.

Flexport books FCL and LCL space through partner carriers, offers US customs brokerage, and supports inland trucking from factory to port as well as delivery to a US warehouse. Ocean freight, customs, and inland transport usually appear as separate charges, so add them up to find your total landed cost. The model suits mid-size and larger importers who like working in a dashboard.

## Maersk

Maersk owns the vessels that cross the Pacific, so importers negotiate directly with the carrier instead of going through a forwarder. The company also offers inland transport, customs-related services, and wider logistics support for full-container programs.

Direct carrier pricing works well for consistent container volume out of Shanghai. Importers with occasional shipments sometimes find sharper per-container rates through a forwarder that pools cargo from many customers. If you book directly with Maersk, line up a separate partner for customs brokerage and last-mile delivery.

## Expeditors

Expeditors is a non-asset-based forwarder with offices in major Chinese manufacturing hubs, including Shanghai. Its China teams work directly with US staff on customs clearance and final delivery.

The company manages filings with US Customs and Border Protection, including classification, valuation, and formal or informal entry. It also runs its own platform for tracking and documentation. Freight, customs clearance, and cargo insurance usually come with separate prices. This detail-heavy approach appeals to importers with compliance-sensitive products, but less so to those who want a quick, simple quote.

## Sinotrans

Headquartered in Beijing, Sinotrans is one of China’s largest integrated logistics networks, with strong ocean and air capacity built around Chinese exporters. It offers warehousing, customs documentation support, and container shipping from Shanghai and other major ports.

Its scale can bring competitive pricing on standard routes for importers with steady container volume. Since Sinotrans focuses on export-side coordination from China, confirm in writing how it will handle US customs brokerage and final-mile delivery for your destination.

## CEVA Logistics

CEVA Logistics provides ocean and air freight, contract logistics, and ground transportation on China-to-USA lanes, with offices supporting cargo out of Shanghai. It also coordinates customs brokerage and offers warehousing and distribution management alongside standard forwarding.

Its contract logistics arm appeals to importers who want the same company to move the container and handle warehousing or fulfillment. CEVA provides pricing through direct quotes. Ask how container delivery transfers to warehouse receiving so one party stays accountable for the last leg.

## C.H. Robinson

C.H. Robinson is a non-asset freight broker. It negotiates container space with ocean carriers on your behalf rather than owning vessels, and its digital tools let importers compare ocean and air options on China-to-USA routes.

The company tracks Trans-Pacific market conditions, including the import spike before the Lunar New Year factory shutdown. Quotes follow current carrier rates, so expect numbers to shift between inquiry and booking. That flexibility suits importers willing to accept rate changes in exchange for broker-negotiated space and flexible routing.

## How We Compared Shanghai FCL Forwarders

We judged each provider using the same four questions and documented service scope, not hands-on testing or performance data. The rankings are editorial judgments, not proof of performance. Verify current Shanghai FCL capabilities with any provider before booking.

### How transparent are quotes about origin, destination, and delivery charges?

A clear quote separates origin charges, ocean freight, destination charges, drayage, and customs fees. Transparent destination charges prevent unexpected terminal handling surprises upon arrival.

Flexport and Expeditors typically list components separately, which gives you detail but means you’ll need to add them up. AEB Logistics describes its approach as one contact, one price, and can provide an itemized breakdown on request.

### Which shipment milestones are visible, and who handles exceptions?

Look for named milestones such as Export Customs, Export Departure, Import Entry, and Import Arrival, along with a named person who owns problems when they come up. AEB assigns a dedicated account manager. Flexport routes questions through a messaging system, while Maersk, as a carrier, tracks the container leg itself.

### How much of the factory-to-door service does each provider coordinate?

Full coverage means factory pickup, export clearance, ocean carriage, US entry, drayage, and delivery. Forwarders such as AEB, Kuehne+Nagel, and CEVA position themselves around that full chain. Maersk and Sinotrans may leave US brokerage or final-mile delivery to a separate partner, so confirm each handoff.

### What demonstrates Shanghai-to-USA route expertise?

Useful signals include China-based teams, named Shanghai handling, and leaders with carrier experience. Alan Fan’s 20-plus years in China shipping is one example, separate from AEB’s 2012 founding date. Ask any forwarder how many Shanghai FCL loads it books and which US ports it uses most.

## How to Compare Quotes for Your Shanghai FCL Shipment

Quotes only compare fairly when they cover the same scope. Start by putting every offer on the same footing: same container, same terms, same start and end points. The [best China to USA FCL forwarders compared](https://aeblogistics.com/best-china-to-usa-fcl-forwarders-compared/) page extends this approach across more origin ports.

### What cargo details, container size, and Incoterms should you provide?

Send the origin and destination, Incoterms such as FOB or EXW, HS code, cargo value, box count, cubic meters, kilograms, and cargo-ready date. Providing an initial commercial invoice and packing list ensures accurate freight quotes across different providers.

Specify whether you need a 20-foot container, a 40-foot container, or a 40-foot high-cube container. Accurate weights and descriptions help prevent unexpected adjustments to freight rates after booking.

### Does the quote cover port-to-port or door-to-door transportation?

State the start and end point on every quote. Port-to-port covers only the ocean leg. Door-to-door adds pickup, export clearance, import entry, drayage, and delivery, but it doesn’t automatically mean DDP. Read the [DDP shipping](https://aeblogistics.com/ddp-shipping/) explanation to see when duties are included.

### Who handles customs entry, the customs bond, duties, and taxes?

Importers remain responsible for duties and taxes as the importer of record. Ask who files the entry, who secures the US customs bond (annual or single-entry), and whether you need a Power of Attorney.

AEB lists brokerage at around USD 125, depending on commodity count, while other providers price brokerage separately.

### Which exclusions, free-time limits, and delivery accessorials could increase your cost?

Check for port congestion fees, detention and demurrage after free time, chassis charges, and delivery extras. AEB may charge USD 100 for liftgate service, and residential deliveries carry additional charges. Ask for the free days at the terminal and receiving warehouse in writing.

### How should you weigh total landed cost against transit time and delivery requirements?

Compare total landed cost, not just the ocean rate. A cheaper rate that excludes drayage, brokerage, and free-time exposure can end up costing more. AEB’s published door-to-door ocean estimate is about 30 to 40 days, though actual times vary with customs, port congestion, weather, and season. Match that window to your Amazon delivery appointment or warehouse receiving date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Shanghai FCL forwarder is a good fit for a small US importer?

AEB Logistics suits small importers and FBA sellers who want one account manager and customs coordination handled in one place. Flexport suits those who prefer a dashboard. Large enterprise networks usually fit higher-volume programs better.

### What is the average cost of shipping a full container from Shanghai to the USA?

Current freight rates vary with the market, container size, destination port, and season, so there’s no single figure that holds for long.

Request itemized freight quotes that detail origin, ocean, destination charges, drayage, and customs fees. Comparing quotes from experienced Shanghai shipping agents gives you the clearest picture of your overall China to USA shipping costs.

### Should I use a freight forwarder or book directly with Maersk?

Booking with Maersk can suit importers with regular container volume who can handle customs brokerage and delivery themselves. A forwarder coordinates those extra steps and may pool cargo for smaller shippers. Your volume and in-house logistics skills will help determine which route is simpler.

### How long does Shanghai-to-USA FCL shipping take door to door?

AEB Logistics estimates about 30 to 40 days for ocean freight door to door. Customs processing, port congestion, weather, carrier schedules, and seasonal demand can affect the timing. Treat any estimate as a planning range, not a guarantee.

### Can these forwarders deliver full-container shipments to Amazon FBA?

AEB Logistics supports Amazon FBA shipping with labeling, palletization, shipment splitting, and appointment scheduling through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal. For the other providers, confirm their door-to-door shipping capabilities and FBA experience before you book.

### Does a door-to-door FCL quote include US customs duties and taxes?

Not automatically. Door-to-door describes the route, while DDP explains who pays duties at delivery. Importers remain responsible for duties and taxes, and Amazon won’t accept FBA shipments with unpaid duties, taxes, or shipping charges.
