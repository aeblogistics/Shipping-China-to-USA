## FCL and LCL Ocean Freight Management

Ocean freight remains the cost-efficient default for bulk inventory, and the FCL versus LCL decision comes down to volume. Full Container Load (FCL) gives you a dedicated 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container, while Less than Container Load (LCL) consolidates your cargo with other shippers’ goods in a shared container.

Recent sea freight pricing put 20GP containers at $5,400 to $6,600 and 40HQ containers at $6,075 to $7,425 to major US ports, with LCL running around $130 per cubic meter, according to [this freight rate comparison](https://www.freightright.com/solution/freight-forwarder-china-to-us) and market indexes on Freightos. Shipments under roughly 15 cubic meters usually cost less through LCL; larger volumes tend to favor FCL on a per-unit basis.

Managing total shipping costs also requires guarding against port delays. Unplanned dwell time can quickly trigger expensive demurrage at container terminals or detention fees for holding equipment past free time.

A forwarder handling both modes lets you switch between them as order sizes change, without re-negotiating with a new provider. AEB Logistics offers factory pickup, customs clearance coordination, and cargo insurance coordination across both FCL and [LCL delivery](https://aeblogistics.com/lcl-delivery/), plus multi-supplier consolidation where your goods come from more than one factory. Ask any forwarder for door-to-door pricing on both options before locking in a shipping mode.

## Air Freight and Express Replenishment Options

Air freight buys you speed when an Amazon listing is about to go out of stock. A shipment from Hong Kong to Los Angeles can take around five days by air, compared with at least three weeks by ocean freight, and general FBA guidance puts standard air freight lead times at roughly 7 to 10 days depending on destination.

Express courier services add another tier above that: current rates run about $17.11 per kilogram for 2 to 4 day door-to-door delivery with built-in customs clearance, versus roughly $7.40 per kilogram for standard air freight over 1,000 kg, according to [this air freight cost breakdown](https://www.freightright.com/solution/freight-forwarder-china-to-us). Air costs more per kilogram, so it works best for urgent restocks or high-value SKUs where a stockout costs more than the freight premium.

Air carriers price shipments according to chargeable weight. This billable figure is determined by comparing gross weight against volumetric weight, making efficient packaging essential to avoid paying for empty space.

AEB Logistics builds air freight into its FBA forwarding, including packing coordination, customs clearance, and delivery straight to fulfillment centers. Keeping one forwarder for both air and ocean means you can shift modes mid-season without re-quoting documentation from scratch. ## Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) Quote Transparency

DDP shipping puts duties, taxes, and delivery under one invoice, and the forwarder’s transparency around that invoice tells you a lot about how the rest of the relationship will run. Under Delivered Duty Paid terms, the seller or forwarder covers freight, customs clearance, duty payment, and final delivery to your address, compared with FOB or CIF terms where you handle customs and inland transport yourself.

DDP costs more upfront than port-to-port pricing, but it removes the guesswork of tracking separate invoices from a carrier, a customs broker, and a trucking company. The trade-off only pays off if the quote itemizes what is actually included.

AEB Logistics states it avoids sneaky surcharges and can provide an itemized charge breakdown on request, which is the kind of documentation worth asking for before you book. Confirm in writing whether duties, customs bond fees, and destination handling are baked into the DDP price or billed separately later. A forwarder unwilling to itemize a [DDP shipping quote](https://aeblogistics.com/ddp-shipping-china-to-usa/) is one to question further before committing inventory funds.

## U.S. Customs Entry and HS/HTS Classification Support

Correct HS/HTS classification determines your duty rate, and getting it wrong causes delays or penalties at the port. Import duty rates vary widely by product category, with electronics facing roughly 2 to 6% and apparel reaching 16 to 20%, based on [this landed cost analysis](https://www.freightright.com/solution/freight-forwarder-china-to-us). Many Chinese imports also incur Section 301 tariffs, which add substantial duty percentages onto designated product lists.

For ocean freight, U.S. Customs and Border Protection enforces strict Importer Security Filing deadlines at least 24 hours prior to origin vessel departure. Filing late or inaccurately risks container holds and steep statutory penalties.

A forwarder’s customs brokerage support should include HS/HTS code guidance, import-entry documentation, and duty and tax estimation once you provide shipment details. U.S. Customs and Border Protection also levies the merchandise processing fee on formal commercial entries. AEB Logistics states customs brokerage can be bundled into a shipment quote for around $125, depending on the number of commodities, and offers customs bond options including use of an AEB bond in applicable cases.

Remember that duties and taxes remain the importer’s responsibility even when a forwarder handles the filing. Amazon does not act as Importer of Record for FBA shipments and will not accept cargo arriving with unpaid duties, taxes, or shipping charges. A Power of Attorney is required whenever a forwarder performs the clearance on your behalf, so ask early which documents you need to sign before cargo departs China.

## Amazon FBA Labeling, Palletization, and Appointment Scheduling

FBA-compliant labeling and packaging determine whether Amazon accepts a shipment on arrival, and getting this wrong at the warehouse stage costs more time than any port delay. Forwarders supporting Amazon FBA typically handle FBA labeling, UPS labeling where a carrier requires it, packaging, and palletization before cargo ever leaves the origin warehouse.

Appointment scheduling adds another layer. Amazon requires delivery windows booked through its Carrier Appointment Request Portal, and missing that window can bounce a truck back to a holding yard.

A complete door-to-door quote should include origin pickup, export customs, ocean or air freight, destination handling, import customs clearance, duties and taxes if DDP, and final delivery. ### How can importers compare total landed cost instead of the lowest freight rate?

Add freight, duties, taxes, insurance, customs brokerage fees, and warehousing or accessorial charges to your product cost, then compare that total across forwarders. A base rate of $5,400 for a 20-foot container can reach $7,200 or more once destination charges and surcharges apply, according to [this landed cost breakdown](https://www.freightright.com/solution/freight-forwarder-china-to-us), so the lowest freight quote isn’t always the cheapest shipment.

### What shipment details reduce quote changes and accessorial charges?

Providing accurate origin and destination addresses, Incoterms (FOB or EXW), HS code, cargo value, box count, cubic meters, weight, and cargo-ready date reduces the chance of a quote changing after booking. Missing or inaccurate details are the most common reason a quote shifts once cargo is already moving.

### Which DDP responsibilities should be confirmed before booking?

Confirm whether the DDP price includes duty payment, customs bond fees, destination drayage, and final delivery, or whether any of those get billed as separate line items later. Also confirm who holds Importer of Record responsibility, since Amazon does not accept FBA shipments arriving with unpaid duties or taxes even under a DDP arrangement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What freight forwarder capabilities matter most for Amazon FBA shipping from China to the USA?

FBA labeling, palletization, appointment scheduling through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal, and shipment splitting across multiple fulfillment centers are the capabilities that most directly affect whether Amazon accepts your inventory on schedule. Customs clearance support and milestone tracking round out the list, since delays at either stage push back your restock timeline.

### How long does freight shipping from China to the USA usually take?

Express courier shipments typically take about 3 to 5 days, standard air freight runs roughly 8 to 10 days, and door-to-door ocean freight takes about 30 to 40 days. Actual timing shifts with customs processing, port congestion, carrier schedules, and seasonal demand.

### Is air freight or ocean freight better for Amazon FBA inventory replenishment?

Air freight suits urgent restocks or high-value SKUs where a stockout costs more than the shipping premium, with transit around 7 to 10 days on standard air freight. Ocean freight fits routine, larger-volume replenishment where a 30 to 40 day door-to-door window fits your inventory planning.

### What does DDP shipping from China to the USA include?

Delivered Duty Paid shipping covers origin pickup, ocean or air freight, U.S. customs clearance, duty and tax payment, and final delivery to your address under one invoice. Confirm in writing which specific fees are bundled into the DDP price, since itemized breakdowns vary by forwarder.

### What documents are needed for U.S. customs clearance when importing from China?

A commercial invoice with accurate HS codes and cargo values, a packing list, a bill of lading or air waybill, and a Power of Attorney authorizing your forwarder to file on your behalf are the core documents. Importers frequently also supply a certificate of origin to prove where manufacturing occurred. Depending on the product category, additional certifications such as FDA or FCC documentation may also apply.

### Can a freight forwarder split one shipment across multiple Amazon FBA warehouses?

Yes, forwarders offering FBA-specific services can split inventory from a single shipment across multiple Amazon fulfillment centers based on Amazon’s assigned distribution plan. This requires accurate box-level labeling and coordination with Amazon’s booking system before the cargo leaves the origin warehouse.
