## Written Ocean Freight Routing and Carrier Booking Plan

A reliable forwarder puts your routing plan in writing before booking a single container. That means naming the origin port in China, the destination port in the USA, expected transit times, and which ocean carrier will handle the move.

Verbal promises about “a fast boat” or “no problem, it’ll get there” are not a routing plan. Ask for the vessel booking confirmation once it’s issued, since this document shows the actual carrier, vessel name, and sailing schedule your cargo is assigned to.

For FCL (Full Container Load) shipments, this also means confirming container type, whether 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube, since the wrong container size can mean repacking cargo or paying for unused space. For LCL (Less than Container Load) shipments, ask how your cargo will be consolidated with other shippers’ goods and which port it will be deconsolidated at.

A forwarder who can produce this routing detail on request, rather than after you’ve already paid a deposit, is showing you real operational control over your shipment, not just a sales quote.

## ISF 10+2 Filing Readiness Before Vessel Departure

ISF 10+2, also called the “Importer Security Filing,” must reach U.S. Customs and Border Protection 24 hours before your cargo is loaded onto the vessel in China. Missing this deadline can trigger CBP penalties and cargo holds once the ship arrives, so a forwarder’s ISF process is one of the clearest early tests of reliability.

The filing requires 10 data elements from the importer (things like seller, buyer, and manufacturer information) plus 2 from the carrier (vessel stow plan and container status). A dependable forwarder asks for this information early, well before the cargo-ready date, instead of scrambling once the vessel is already booked.

Ask your forwarder directly: “When do you need my ISF information, and how will you confirm it was filed on time?” A forwarder who can answer with a specific timeline and a confirmation process is signaling real customs readiness. One who shrugs off the question or treats ISF as an afterthought is a warning sign worth taking seriously before your cargo ever reaches the port.

## Commercial Invoice and Packing List Review

A reliable forwarder reviews your commercial invoice and packing list before your cargo departs, not after it arrives at a U.S. port. These two documents form the backbone of your customs entry, and errors here are one of the most common causes of clearance delays.

The commercial invoice needs to match what’s actually being shipped: accurate product descriptions, unit values, total value, country of origin, and buyer and seller details. The packing list needs to line up with it, showing carton counts, weights, and dimensions that match the actual cargo.

A good forwarder catches mismatches early. If your supplier’s invoice says “plastic goods” when the shipment is actually silicone kitchenware, that vague description can trigger a customs hold or a request for more information from CBP.

Ask your forwarder if they review these documents before booking or only after the cargo has sailed. A forwarder who checks early gives you time to fix supplier paperwork errors while the goods are still in China, which is far easier than correcting them mid-transit.

## HS/HTS Classification and Duty Estimate Support

A dependable forwarder helps you confirm the correct HS/HTS code for your product before shipment. This code determines duty rates, Section 301 tariffs, and whether cargo qualifies for the Section 321 de minimis exemption for low-value parcels.

AEB Logistics provides HS/HTS code guidance and documentation support, along with import duty and tax estimation support when shipment information is provided. That kind of upfront classification help lets you calculate total landed cost with precision before goods leave the factory.

Ask your forwarder to walk through the classification logic for your specific product, not just hand you a number. A forwarder who can explain why a code applies, and flag categories prone to anti-dumping or countervailing duties, is doing real compliance work on your behalf.

Keep in mind that even with forwarder support, [importers remain responsible](https://aeblogistics.com/customs-brokerage/) for the accuracy of the classification and for paying applicable duties and taxes.

## Customs Bond, Cargo Insurance, and Power of Attorney Guidance

A trustworthy forwarder explains your customs bond options clearly instead of bundling them into a quote without context. A customs bond is a financial guarantee to US Customs and Border Protection that duties and fees will be paid, with options for single-entry or continuous bonds.

Beyond bonds, a dependable partner also arranges comprehensive cargo insurance to protect goods against damage or loss during transit. AEB Logistics can arrange an annual bond for U.S. imports or offer use of an AEB bond on a case-by-case basis, depending on the shipment.

A forwarder should walk you through which bond and insurance coverage fits your shipping frequency and cargo value. This ensures you avoid being underinsured against maritime and logistical risks.

A Power of Attorney (POA) is also required whenever you want your forwarder to handle customs clearance on your behalf. This document authorizes them to file entries and communicate with CBP as your representative.

Ask exactly what the POA covers and how long it stays active. A reliable partner explains this plainly, without burying it in fine print, and confirms in writing which bond type applies to your shipment before cargo departs China.

## Bill of Lading, Arrival Notice, and Cargo Release Visibility

A dependable forwarder gives you visibility into three key documents: the bill of lading, the arrival notice, and the cargo release confirmation. The bill of lading is the contract of carriage and proof that your cargo was loaded onto the vessel.

The arrival notice signals when the vessel reaches port and demurrage clocks begin. Cargo release confirmation shows that customs has cleared your shipment so it can move inland.

## Port Drayage, Transload, and Final-Mile Delivery Coordination

A reliable forwarder coordinates the physical handoffs after your container clears customs, from port drayage to final delivery. Drayage means trucking the container a short distance, usually from the port to a nearby warehouse or rail yard.

Transload is the process of moving cargo out of an ocean container and into a domestic truck trailer, often used when cargo needs to be split for multiple destinations or when a container can’t legally travel inland due to weight limits. A forwarder who names actual carrier relationships, instead of describing trucking in vague terms, is showing you a real operational network rather than a promise.

     ### Which charges should appear separately in an ocean freight quote?

Ocean freight, customs clearance, drayage, and accessorial charges like liftgate service should each appear as their own line item. A quote that bundles these into one number makes it hard to spot what’s missing, and missing items tend to show up later as unplanned charges.

### How can importers compare DDP, FOB, and EXW proposals fairly?

Confirm which Incoterm each quote is based on before comparing dollar figures. DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) includes duties and delivery in one price, while FOB (Free on Board) and EXW (Ex Works) shift more cost and responsibility onto the importer for inland freight, customs, or both, so a lower FOB quote isn’t automatically cheaper once those gaps are filled in.

### Which missing details can create unexpected charges after booking?

Inaccurate weights, dimensions, HS codes, or cargo-ready dates are common causes of quote discrepancies once a shipment is actually booked. Providing accurate box counts, cubic meters, kilograms, and product descriptions upfront, along with your Incoterms and cargo value, helps prevent a quote from changing once your cargo is already in transit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I choose a reliable freight forwarder from China to the USA?

Look for a forwarder who can document ISF filing timing, provide an itemized quote on request, and explain HS/HTS classification for your product before booking. A dedicated account manager and clear shipment tracking through customs milestones are practical signs the company can handle problems if they come up mid-shipment.

### How long does sea freight from China to the USA usually take?

Ocean freight door to door generally runs about 30 to 40 days, though this depends on the origin port, destination, and customs processing. Port congestion, carrier schedules, and seasonal demand can extend that window, so build in a buffer when planning inventory timing.

### What documents does a freight forwarder need for a U.S. import shipment?

A forwarder needs your commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and ISF 10+2 filing information at minimum. For customs clearance, a Power of Attorney is also required if you want the forwarder to file the entry on your behalf.

### Should I choose LCL, FCL, or express courier for my shipment?

For small, urgent sample shipments under 150 kg, an express courier offers the fastest delivery and simplified customs clearance. For larger cargo, LCL ocean freight is cost-effective for partial loads, while FCL provides dedicated container space and faster port processing.

### Can a freight forwarder deliver goods from China directly to an Amazon FBA warehouse?

Yes, a forwarder experienced in Amazon FBA logistics can handle factory pickup through delivery to an FBA fulfillment center, including FBA labeling and appointment scheduling. Confirm the forwarder can schedule delivery appointments through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal and manage shipment splitting if your inventory needs to go to multiple warehouses.
