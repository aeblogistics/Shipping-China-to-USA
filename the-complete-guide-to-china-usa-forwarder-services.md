## What Does a Freight Forwarder Do for U.S.-Bound Cargo?

A freight forwarder manages the physical and paperwork side of moving your cargo from a Chinese factory to its final U.S. destination. Instead of contacting carriers, brokers, and truckers separately, you work through one coordinator who handles factory pickup, booking, documentation, and delivery as a connected process.

Freight forwarding services typically include booking ocean or air carriers, securing cargo insurance, preparing export documents, and coordinating customs clearance. They also schedule inland trucking on the U.S. side to complete delivery.

The forwarder acts as your project manager for international logistics, tracking your shipment from supplier pickup through final delivery, a role described in [this explanation of how freight forwarders operate between China and the U.S.](https://mindensourcing.com/how-a-freight-forwarder-works-from-china-to-the-usa/).

### From Factory Pickup to Final Delivery

Your forwarder should coordinate directly with your supplier to schedule pickup once cargo is ready. This includes confirming carton counts, packaging condition, and pickup timing with the factory, so nothing sits waiting at the dock.

From there, the shipment moves through export customs, ocean or air transport, import clearance, and inland delivery. A single point of contact reduces the risk of miscommunication between these handoffs, particularly when your cargo is routed through multiple parties.

### How Forwarders Work With Carriers, Brokers, and Truckers

Forwarders book cargo space with ocean and air carriers, but they don’t typically operate their own ships, planes, or trucks. Instead, they coordinate with carrier partners and truck freight operators to move your freight.

For example, AEB Logistics works with carriers including Schneider, J.B. Hunt, and UPS Freight for inland delivery across domestic distribution networks.

Customs brokerage is a related but distinct function. As [this breakdown of forwarder versus broker roles](https://fulfillmen.com/freight-forwarder/) puts it, the freight forwarder gets your cargo to the customs border while the broker gets it through. Many forwarders bundle brokerage into one quote so you have a single point of accountability.

### When Door-to-Door Delivery Is the Right Service Level

Door-to-door shipping covers the full route from your supplier’s factory to your final U.S. address, including inland trucking on both ends. This works well for importers who want one quote and one responsible party for the entire journey.

Businesses with in-house customs expertise or existing trucking relationships sometimes prefer a narrower scope, like port-to-port only. For most SMB importers and Amazon sellers, door-to-door reduces coordination work and the number of vendors you need to manage. ## Which Shipping Method Fits Your Cargo and Deadline?

Your shipping method choice comes down to three factors: how fast you need the cargo, how much it weighs and costs, and your budget tolerance. Ocean freight costs less per unit but takes weeks; air freight costs more but arrives in days; express courier sits at the fastest and most expensive end for small parcels and samples.

### Air Freight for Urgent and High-Value Goods

Air freight suits shipments where speed matters more than per-unit cost, such as new product launches or replenishment orders that can’t wait. An air shipment from Hong Kong to Los Angeles can take around five days, while ocean freight for the same route takes at least three weeks, according to AEB Logistics. General industry guidance for standard air freight puts transit closer to 8 to 10 days depending on the specific route and destination.

Air freight works well for high-value goods where the added transport cost is small relative to product value, and for shipments sensitive to spoilage, damage, or seasonal deadlines.

### Ocean Freight With FCL or LCL

Ocean freight remains the most economical option for bulk cargo and heavier bulk shipments. Full Container Load (FCL) gives you a dedicated 20ft container, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container, which suits higher cargo volume or goods needing extra security.

Less than Container Load (LCL) lets you share container space with other shippers, paying only for the space your cargo occupies. This provides an economical fit when your order doesn’t fill a full container.

Ocean rates vary by route and season. As a reference point, LCL rates have run between $45 and $85 per CBM (cubic meter), with a standard 20ft container priced around $1,400 to the West Coast, according to [this sea freight cost breakdown](https://fasarylogistics.com/sea-shipping-from-china-to-usa/). Rates fluctuate with fuel costs, port capacity, and demand, so treat any quote as a snapshot rather than a fixed number.

### Express Courier for Samples and Small Parcels

Express shipping through carriers like DHL, FedEx, and UPS works best for samples, small parcels, or urgent low-volume shipments. These express courier services typically move in 3 to 5 days.

However, express shipping carries the highest per-kilogram cost of any method, making it impractical for full inventory restocks.

### How to Set Realistic Transit-Time Expectations

Published transit estimates are starting points, not guarantees. AEB Logistics cites general benchmarks of about 3 to 5 days for express courier, 8 to 10 days for standard air freight, and roughly 30 to 40 days for door-to-door ocean freight from China to the USA.

Port congestion, customs holds, carrier schedule changes, and seasonal demand spikes (like pre-holiday shipping surges) can extend any of these windows. Build buffer time into your inventory planning rather than ordering restocks based on best-case transit times alone.

## How Much Does China-to-USA Freight Really Cost?

Your real shipping cost includes far more than the quoted freight rate per container or per kilogram. Base rates get the headlines, but destination charges, customs duties, and accessorial fees determine what you actually pay to get cargo delivered.

### How Weight, CBM, and Chargeable Weight Affect Freight Rates

Ocean freight is typically priced by cubic meter (CBM) for LCL shipments, while air freight uses chargeable weight, the greater of actual weight or volumetric weight. Volumetric weight is calculated from the dimensions of your cargo, so bulky but lightweight products can cost more to ship by air than their actual weight suggests.

Getting accurate box counts, CBM, and kilogram figures to your forwarder before requesting a quote helps you avoid pricing surprises once cargo is measured at pickup.

### Freight Charges to Compare Beyond the Base Rate

A port-to-port ocean quote can look attractive until you add destination charges. Common line items include fuel surcharges, terminal handling charges, and container drayage on the U.S. side. Destination CFS (container freight station) fees, chassis charges, and final delivery can double the landed cost when a quote stops at the port instead of your actual delivery address, as noted in [this analysis of sea freight pricing traps](https://fasarylogistics.com/sea-shipping-from-china-to-usa/).

Ask any forwarder for a complete breakdown that includes origin charges, ocean or air freight, destination handling, customs brokerage, and final-mile delivery, not just the base transport line.

### How to Calculate Total Landed Cost

Total landed cost adds your product cost, freight charges, customs duties and taxes, cargo insurance, and any warehousing or final-mile fees into one number. This is the figure that actually determines your margin, not the freight quote alone.

For example, a $1,400 FCL rate to the West Coast looks different once you add customs brokerage (commonly around $125 depending on commodity count), duties based on your HS code, and last-mile trucking to your warehouse or Amazon facility. Calculate landed cost per unit, not just per shipment, so you can compare it against your selling price.

### How to Spot Hidden Fees in a Shipping Quote

Ask for an itemized quote before you book, and request confirmation in writing about what’s included versus billed separately. Common gaps include liftgate service for locations without a loading dock (a stated $100 fee at some carriers), residential delivery surcharges, and storage fees if cargo sits at a warehouse awaiting pickup.

A transparent forwarder should be willing to provide a full charge breakdown on request rather than folding accessorial fees into vague line items. AEB Logistics states its approach as avoiding sneaky surcharges, with itemized breakdowns available when requested; treat that transparency standard as a benchmark when comparing quotes from other providers.

## Customs Documents, Duties, and Importer Responsibilities

Handling US customs clearance requires accurate documentation and a clear understanding of who is responsible for duties and taxes. Navigating US customs smoothly ensures cargo avoids hold-ups at the border.

As the importer, you remain legally responsible for these obligations even when a forwarder or broker handles the paperwork on your behalf.

### Core Documents for Customs Clearance

Every shipment needs a commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading at minimum. Depending on the product and origin claims, you may also need a certificate of origin.

Accurate product descriptions, correct declared value, and complete documentation prevent the kind of delays described in [this guide to customs clearance for China-to-USA shipments](https://dfhlogistics.com/customs-clearance-shipping-from-china-to-usa/).

Missing or inconsistent paperwork is one of the most common causes of held cargo and intrusive customs inspections at U.S. ports.

### HS Codes, Declared Value, and Duties and Taxes

Your HS code (Harmonized System code) determines the duty rate CBP applies to your goods, so getting the classification right protects you from both underpayment penalties and overpayment. AEB Logistics offers HS/HTS code guidance and duty and tax estimation support when given accurate shipment details.

Import duties and import taxes are calculated based on declared value and product classification. Verify your product’s HS code with your broker or forwarder before shipping, since misclassification can trigger customs review or incorrect duty assessments.

### ISF 10+2 and Customs Bonds for Ocean Imports

Ocean shipments to the U.S. require an Importer Security Filing (ISF), sometimes called ISF 10+2, submitted before cargo loads at the origin port. Missing this filing can result in penalties or cargo holds.

A customs bond is also required for formal entries. AEB Logistics can arrange an annual bond or offer use of its own AEB bond on a case-by-case basis for qualifying shipments, and a Power of Attorney is required when a forwarder performs customs clearance on your behalf.

### DDP, FOB, EXW, and CIF: Choosing Incoterms Carefully

Your Incoterms determine who is responsible for freight, insurance, and customs at each stage of the journey. Under EXW (Ex Works), you take on responsibility from the supplier’s door onward. Under FOB (Free on Board), the supplier handles origin costs up to the port. Under CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight), the supplier arranges the main transport and insurance.

DDP shipping (Delivered Duty Paid) shifts duty and tax payment to the seller or forwarder, with the price delivered to your door already including these costs. Importers buying via DDP shipping have fewer administrative hurdles because the forwarder manages customs clearance and payment.

For direct parcel imports, shipments valued under $800 may enter duty-free under the de minimis exemption. However, commercial freight restocks do not qualify for de minimis treatment.

For Amazon FBA shipments specifically, keep in mind that Amazon does not act as Importer of Record and will not accept shipments arriving with unpaid duties, taxes, or shipping charges. Confirm which Incoterm your quote assumes before comparing prices between forwarders, since a low FOB quote and a low DDP quote are not directly comparable.

## How to Manage Multiple Suppliers Before Export

Consolidating cargo from multiple Chinese suppliers into a single shipment reduces the number of separate freight charges, customs entries, and delivery appointments you manage. Instead of paying for several partial LCL shipments, warehouse consolidation combines your cartons into one container or one shared LCL booking.

### When Warehouse Consolidation Reduces Complexity

If you buy from more than one factory or trading company, routing cargo to a consolidation warehouse in China before export can simplify both cost and paperwork. Your forwarder receives cartons from each supplier, verifies counts against your packing list, and combines everything into a single outbound shipment.

This approach works especially well when suppliers are in different cities or when order timing varies. Waiting for the last supplier’s cargo to arrive at the consolidation point before booking freight avoids paying for multiple partial shipments.

### Preparing Cartons, Labels, and Shipping Documents

Track supplier names, product lists, carton counts, dimensions, and weights carefully as goods arrive at the consolidation warehouse. Each supplier’s cartons need consistent labeling so nothing gets lost or misattributed once combined into one shipment.

Your commercial invoice and packing list should reflect the combined shipment accurately, listing goods by supplier or product line as needed for customs review. Inconsistent labeling between suppliers is one of the more common causes of confusion at the consolidation stage.

### Inspection, Palletization, and Cargo-Readiness Controls

Before export, confirm quality inspection and palletization needs with your forwarder. Inspecting cargo at the consolidation warehouse, rather than after it lands in the U.S., gives you a chance to catch damaged or incorrect goods before they take up container space.

AEB Logistics includes packaging and palletization support alongside inventory management, which helps when consolidated cargo needs to be split later for delivery to multiple U.S. destinations or Amazon warehouses. Confirm with your forwarder how they document cargo condition at each handoff point, since this record matters if a claim comes up later.

## Final-Mile Planning for Warehouses, Retail, and Amazon FBA

Final-mile delivery is where inventory disruptions most often happen, since delivery requirements vary by destination type and a missed appointment can delay stocking by days. Planning this stage before you book freight prevents costly last-minute scrambles.

### Delivery Requirements for U.S. Warehouses and Facilities

Commercial warehouses and 3PL facilities often require advance delivery appointments, specific pallet configurations, or dock-height trucks. Confirm your destination’s receiving hours, appointment system, and any labeling requirements with your forwarder before cargo arrives at the U.S. port, as outlined in [this guide to warehouse delivery planning from China](https://honourocean.com/usa-warehouse-delivery-from-china/).

### Amazon FBA Labels, Appointment Scheduling, and Shipment Splitting

Amazon FBA shipments have specific labeling requirements, including FBA labels and sometimes UPS labeling depending on the carrier. Delivery appointments get scheduled through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal, and for LCL shipments, this typically happens after goods arrive at the forwarder’s destination-country warehouse.

Shipment splitting across multiple Amazon fulfillment centers adds another layer of coordination, since your forwarder needs to divide inventory and schedule separate appointments for each warehouse. ### LTL, Small Parcel, and Accessorial Delivery Costs

Last-mile delivery to Amazon or other U.S. destinations typically runs through LTL (less-than-truckload) carriers or small parcel delivery (SPD), depending on shipment size. LTL suits palletized freight, while SPD works for smaller individual cartons going to fulfillment centers.

Ask your forwarder to itemize accessorial charges upfront, including liftgate, residential delivery, and any storage fees if cargo needs to wait before an Amazon appointment slot opens.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I choose a freight forwarder from China to the USA?

Compare forwarders on quote transparency, customs brokerage experience, warehouse coverage, and communication structure rather than price alone. Ask for an itemized quote that separates freight, customs, and final-mile charges, and confirm whether you get a dedicated account manager or a shared support queue.

### How long does sea shipping from China to the USA take?

Ocean freight, door to door, generally takes around 30 to 40 days, though port congestion, customs holds, and carrier schedules can extend this. An ocean shipment from Hong Kong to Los Angeles alone can take at least three weeks before accounting for inland delivery.

### Is air freight or sea freight cheaper for my shipment?

Sea freight is cheaper per unit for bulk or heavier cargo, while air freight costs more but delivers in days instead of weeks. Air freight makes more sense for urgent, high-value, or relatively small shipments where speed offsets the higher cost.

### What documents do I need to import goods from China into the USA?

You need a commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading at minimum, plus a certificate of origin if applicable. Ocean shipments also require an Importer Security Filing (ISF) submitted before the cargo loads, and formal entries require a customs bond.

### Can a forwarder consolidate shipments from multiple Chinese suppliers?

Yes, forwarders can combine cartons from different suppliers into one shipment through warehouse consolidation in China. This reduces the number of separate freight charges and customs entries compared to shipping each supplier’s goods independently.

### Does door-to-door shipping include customs duties and taxes?

Door-to-door shipping covers transport and delivery, but duties and taxes depend on your Incoterm. DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) includes duties and taxes in the quoted price, while other terms like FOB or EXW leave you responsible for paying them separately as the importer.
