Choosing between port-to-port, [door-to-door](https://aeblogistics.com/door-to-door-shipping-china-to-usa-case-study/), DDP, and [Amazon FBA](https://aeblogistics.com/fba-fulfillment-by-amazon/) shipping can seriously impact your total cost and risk for every shipment from China. Each option shifts tasks like customs clearance, trucking, and final delivery onto you or your [freight forwarder](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-from-china-to-usa/).

**For most importers, it really comes down to how much control you want over customs and delivery versus how much you want your freight forwarder to handle.**

If you run an e-commerce brand or manage inbound freight for a warehouse, you need a model that fits your team’s time and your budget. Port-to-port shipping costs less, but you have to manage customs and trucking yourself.

Door-to-door and [DDP shipping](https://ddp2usa.com/door-to-door-vs-port-to-port-china-usa/) hand off more of that work, which can save you time but usually costs more per shipment.

This guide breaks down how each delivery model works and what it means for your customs compliance. You’ll also see how [international shipping logistics](https://elogy.io/en/incoterms-guide/) factor into your decision.

That way, you can match the right option to your shipment size, your timeline, and your comfort level with [customs paperwork](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-from-china-to-usa-essential-customs-strategies/).

## Key Takeaways

- Your choice of shipping model changes who handles customs, trucking, and final delivery.
- Matching your shipment size and timeline to the right delivery model can lower your costs and reduce delays.
- Amazon FBA shipments often need extra customs and labeling controls that differ from standard warehouse deliveries.
 
## Port-to-Port, Door-to-Door, and DDP at a Glance

Each [shipping method](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-shipping-from-china-to-usa/) covers a different part of the journey from the departure port in China to your final destination in the U.S. The right choice just depends on how much control you want over customs clearance, duties and taxes, and the [last leg of delivery](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-us/).

### What Port-to-Port Service Includes and Excludes

Port-to-port shipping covers [ocean freight](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-the-usa/) from the departure port to the destination port. That’s it.

You have to handle everything before and after yourself. This means arranging your own trucking to the origin port in China, and on the U.S. side, dealing with [import customs clearance](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarders-china-to-usa-shipping-with-full-customs-support/), paying duties and taxes, and setting up drayage to move the container out of the terminal.

If you already have a customs broker and a trucking company lined up, this method works well. Port charges and terminal handling charges at both ends usually show up as separate line items, not tucked into your quote.

Here’s what’s typically excluded:

- Inland trucking to the origin port
- Export documentation
- Import customs clearance
- Duties and taxes
- Final delivery to your warehouse
 
### How Door-to-Door Shipping Extends to the Final Destination

Door-to-door shipping picks up where port-to-port stops. It covers the full route: pickup at your supplier’s factory, road freight to the port, ocean or air freight, and delivery to your final destination in the U.S.

This method cuts down the number of vendors you have to manage. Instead of juggling a trucker in China, an ocean carrier, a customs broker, and a U.S. trucking company, [one provider](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-from-china-to-the-usa/) handles the whole thing from start to finish.

But door-to-door doesn’t always include duties and import clearance. You still need to check your contract for those details.

Some door-to-door quotes only cover transportation and leave customs formalities as a separate line item, so read the agreement closely before you book.

### What Delivered Duty Paid Means in Practice

Delivered Duty Paid, or DDP, is an Incoterm, not a transportation type. Under DDP, your supplier or freight forwarder takes on full responsibility for the shipment, including import customs clearance, duties, and taxes, all the way to your named place.

This is the most hands-off option for you as the buyer. You won’t have to deal with customs paperwork or surprise duty bills.

DDP terms pop up a lot when U.S. importers want price certainty and fewer moving parts. Because the seller absorbs more risk and cost, DDP shipments often carry a higher quoted price than other Incoterms.

Always confirm what “named place” means in your agreement, since it could be your warehouse, an Amazon FBA center, or another location entirely.

### The Difference Between DDP, DAP, and DDU

DDP, DAP, and DDU all describe risk transfer and cost responsibility, not physical transport routes.

## Choosing the Right Delivery Model for Your Shipment

Your choice between port-to-port, [DDP](https://aeblogistics.com/ddp-freight-forwarder-china-to-usa/), and FBA prep should depend on your team’s [customs experience](https://aeblogistics.com/china-usa-freight-forwarding-8-questions-to-ask-before-choosing-a-provider/), your budget for landed cost predictability, and your shipment size.

The answer also shifts based on whether you ship FCL, [LCL](https://aeblogistics.com/lcl-shipping-from-china/), or air freight, and which U.S. port fits your supply chain.

### When Experienced Importers Benefit From Port-to-Port

Port-to-port shipping works best when you already have a customs broker and a trucking plan on the U.S. side. You control freight terms like **[FOB Shenzhen](https://aeblogistics.com/top-10-differences-between-fob-vs-cif-vs-ddp-shipping-terms/)**, **FCA (Free Carrier)**, or **FAS (Free Alongside Ship)**, which puts you in charge of booking ocean freight and insurance.

This model often costs less than DDP because you aren’t paying a markup for someone else to manage customs. It also gives you more flexibility to negotiate rates directly with carriers.

The tradeoff is [risk](https://aeblogistics.com/ddp-shipping-risk-when-shipping-from-china/). If your paperwork has errors or your HS codes are wrong, delays and extra fees fall on you.

Teams without in-house customs knowledge usually pay more in the end through storage fees and missed deadlines.

### When DDP Provides Better Landed-Cost Predictability

DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) shifts customs duties, taxes, and clearance work to your supplier or freight forwarder. You get a fixed landed cost upfront, which makes budgeting a lot simpler.

This matters most for smaller importers or teams without a broker relationship. You know your total cost before the shipment even leaves **Shenzhen**, **Shanghai**, or **Ningbo**.

Compare this to **EXW (Ex Works)**, **CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight)**, or **CFR (Cost and Freight)**, where you take on more risk and paperwork. DDP costs more per unit, but it removes the guesswork.

If your cargo insurance and customs clearance experience is limited, DDP protects you from unexpected duty bills or compliance mistakes.

### How FCL, LCL, and Air Freight Affect the Decision

Your shipment size drives which delivery model makes sense.

- **FCL (Full Container Load):** Best for shipments over 15 CBM. Lower cost per unit, and you control the full container.
- **LCL (Less than Container Load):** Fits smaller shipments under 15 CBM. You share container space, which adds cost per CBM but avoids paying for unused space.
- **Air freight:** Fastest option, priced by **chargeable weight**, not just actual weight. Use this for urgent restocks or high-value items.
 
For large FCL shipments, port-to-port often works well since you have enough volume to justify managing customs yourself.

Smaller LCL shipments benefit more from DDP, since the fixed-cost structure simplifies planning when margins are tighter.

### Selecting West Coast, East Coast, and Inland Routes

Your destination port affects both cost and transit time. West Coast ports like **LAX** and **LGB** offer faster transit from China, usually 12-16 days by ocean.

East Coast ports add 5-10 more days but can reduce inland trucking costs if your warehouse sits closer to that coast.

If you’re shipping to Amazon FBA warehouses, check which fulfillment centers your inventory routes through before booking. Some sellers split shipments across both coasts to balance speed and cost.

Inland routes matter too. A West Coast port with a long truck haul to a Midwest warehouse might cost more overall than an East Coast port with a shorter final leg.

It’s worth comparing total delivered cost, not just ocean freight rates, when picking your route.

## Customs, Compliance, and Amazon FBA Delivery Controls

Customs paperwork and Amazon’s warehouse rules can stop a shipment even after it clears the port. You need to know who is responsible for duties and filings, what documents CBP expects, and how to avoid an FBA appointment rejection.

### Assigning Importer of Record and Brokerage Responsibilities

Before your goods leave China, you need to decide who’ll act as the [importer of record](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-china-to-usa-best-china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-for-new-importers/) (IOR). Most of the time, the buyer takes on this role for shipments to the U.S. under Incoterms 2020 rules from the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC).

As the IOR, you take legal responsibility for duties, taxes, and following CBP rules. You’ll need a [licensed customs broker](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarders-from-china-to-usa-top-10-specialists-for-complex-customs-clearance/) to file your entry unless you handle it yourself for formal entries.

Many freight forwarders include [brokerage as part](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-10-capabilities-us-importers-should-evaluate/) of their service. Sign a [power of attorney](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-how-to-choose-for-alibaba-imports/) (POA) so your broker can work with CBP on your behalf.

Risk of loss and transfer depends on your chosen trade term. Under DDP, the seller handles export clearance and import formalities, but with other terms, that responsibility shifts to you.

### Preparing Documents for U.S. Customs Clearance

Three documents cause most FBA customs delays: the commercial invoice, packing list, and HS code assignment. Your commercial invoice has to show accurate transaction values—[undervaluing goods on the invoice is customs fraud](https://www.customsvalidator.com/amazon-fba-customs), and CBP can flag this quickly.

Suppliers on Alibaba usually give you a 6-digit HS code, but [your entry requires the full 10-digit HTSUS code](https://www.customsvalidator.com/amazon-fba-customs) to determine duty rates and possible Section 301 tariffs.

Country of origin must match the real manufacturing location, even if the goods ship from somewhere else. Product descriptions need details like brand, model, and use—vague terms just invite extra inspections.

Make sure your packing list matches your invoice in quantities and weights. Discrepancies here are a quick way to slow down US customs clearance.

### Preventing Delays Before an Amazon FBA Appointment

Amazon rejects shipments that miss its labeling and appointment rules, even if CBP hasn’t held your goods. Every carton needs an FBA carton label, and pallets need pallet labels that match your shipment plan.

Common issues that block an FBA appointment include:

- Missing or incorrect carton labels
- Pallet dimensions that are too big for Amazon
- SKU counts that don’t match between your shipment plan and actual inventory
- Products needing relabeling that didn’t get fixed before drop-off
 
If your goods sit in a bonded warehouse or at the port waiting for CBP release, you risk demurrage charges. These fees pile up while your product stays out of stock on Amazon, which hurts your ranking and sales.

### Comparing Door-to-FBA Delivery With U.S. Warehousing

Direct-to-FBA shipping sends your inventory straight from the origin port to Amazon’s fulfillment center. This works if you’re shipping full, uniform cartons that already follow Amazon’s labeling rules.

U.S. warehousing adds a transload step. Your freight arrives at a domestic warehouse, gets checked, relabeled if needed, and split into separate shipments for order fulfillment or multiple FBA locations.

This route fits Amazon sellers who sell on multiple channels or want to hold safety stock outside Amazon’s system.

Shipping terms and delivery methods shift cost, risk, and control between you and your [supplier](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-in-china/). Here are answers to common questions importers ask when comparing port-to-port, DDP, and Amazon FBA options for [China to USA freight forwarding](https://www.dripcapital.com/en-us/resources/blog/incoterms-2026).

### What is the difference between port-to-port, door-to-door, DDP, and Amazon FBA delivery?

[Port-to-port shipping](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china/) moves your goods from a Chinese port to a U.S. port. After that, you handle customs clearance and inland transport.

Door-to-door shipping covers the whole trip, from the factory in China to your address in the U.S. This method sometimes uses DDP terms, but not always.

DDP means “[Delivered Duty Paid](https://aeblogistics.com/ddp-shipping-china-to-usa/).” Your supplier pays for shipping, duties, and taxes, and [delivers the goods](https://www.dhl.com/us-en/home/global-forwarding/help-center/incoterms.html) to your location.

Amazon FBA shipping sends your goods to an Amazon fulfillment center instead of your own warehouse. You still need to decide how the goods get there—port-to-port, DDP, or another method.

### When should an importer choose DDP instead of FOB, CIF, or DAP terms?

Pick DDP when you want your supplier to manage customs clearance and duty payments in the U.S. This is great if you don’t have a customs broker or much import experience.

FOB (Free on Board) makes sense if you want more control over freight costs and carrier choice. You take on risk once goods are on the ship.

CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) adds insurance to FOB, but you still handle U.S. import clearance. DAP (Delivered at Place) puts delivery on your supplier but leaves duty payment to you.

Go with DDP if you want it simple. Choose FOB, CIF, or DAP if you want to manage part of the process yourself and maybe save some money.

### Who is responsible for customs clearance, duties, and taxes under each shipping option?

With port-to-port shipping, you’re responsible for customs clearance, duties, and taxes once goods reach the U.S. port. Your supplier’s job ends when the goods leave the origin port.

Under DDP, your supplier manages customs clearance, duties, and taxes for you. You only get the goods after they clear U.S. customs.

Door-to-door shipping can go either way, depending on your contract. Always confirm in writing who pays duties before your goods leave China.

For Amazon FBA, you or your freight forwarder still need to clear customs before goods reach the fulfillment center. Amazon doesn’t handle import clearance for you—wouldn’t that be nice?

### Is DDP shipping suitable for Amazon FBA shipments from China to the United States?

DDP can work well for Amazon FBA shipments, especially for new sellers or anyone without a U.S. customs broker. Your supplier or forwarder takes care of clearance, which can help avoid port delays.

Many FBA sellers choose DDP because [it lowers the risk of shipment holds](https://forestleopard.com/knowledgedetail/Differences-between-DAP-and-DD) from bad paperwork. That matters, since Amazon has strict delivery windows.

DDP shipments usually cost more than other options because the price includes duties and customs fees up front. Weigh this extra cost against the time you’d spend handling clearance yourself.

### What are the main risks and hidden costs of port-to-port shipping for U.S. importers?

Port-to-port shipping puts you in charge of paperwork and fees once goods land in the U.S. That means duties, taxes, port fees, and inland transport costs.

Delays happen if your customs documents have errors or if goods get flagged for inspection. These delays can lead to storage fees at the port, which add up quickly.

You also need to find a [reliable customs broker](https://aeblogistics.com/10-factors-in-comparing-china-to-usa-forwarders/) and trucking company. If anything in this chain fails, your goods might sit at the port longer than planned, costing you money and time.

### How can U.S. warehousing help manage inventory after goods arrive from China?

[U.S. warehousing](https://aeblogistics.com/warehousing/) gives you a spot to stash goods after customs, before they head off to Amazon FBA or your customers. This setup helps you avoid scrambling to ship everything at once.

You get more control over your inventory levels. That flexibility can make a huge difference when things get hectic.

Warehouses can split shipments for FBA, since Amazon usually wants goods sent to several fulfillment centers. If you work with a warehouse near a big port, you might save on both trucking time and costs.

During peak shipping seasons, warehousing gives you a buffer. You can store extra stock ahead of time, instead of crossing your fingers and waiting for new shipments from China.
