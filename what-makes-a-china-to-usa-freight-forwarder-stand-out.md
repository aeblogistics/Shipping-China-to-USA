## Documented ISF Filing Confirmation Before Vessel Departure

Ask for written confirmation that your ISF was filed on time, not just a verbal assurance that “it’s handled.” ISF stands for Importer Security Filing, commonly referenced as ISF 10+2, and it must reach US Customs and Border Protection at least 24 hours before your cargo is loaded onto the vessel in China.

The filing needs 10 data points from you (seller, buyer, manufacturer, and similar details) plus 2 from the ocean carrier (vessel stow plan and container status). Missing the deadline can trigger CBP penalties and cargo holds once the ship reaches a U.S. port.

A forwarder with a real process asks for your ISF data early, well before your cargo-ready date, and sends confirmation once the filing is accepted. If a forwarder can’t tell you when they need this information or how they’ll confirm it was filed, treat that as a warning sign, since [reliable China-to-USA forwarders](https://aeblogistics.com/what-makes-a-china-to-usa-freight-forwarder-reliable/) build ISF readiness into their standard workflow, not as an afterthought.

## HS/HTS Classification Support for Every Imported Product

Getting your HS codes and HTS codes right before shipment protects your landed cost and keeps your cargo out of a customs review. These codes determine your duty rate, whether Section 301 tariffs apply, and whether your shipment qualifies for the Section 321 de minimis exemption on low-value parcels.

A dependable forwarder walks through the classification logic for your specific product rather than handing you a number with no explanation. AEB Logistics provides [HS/HTS code guidance](https://aeblogistics.com/hts-code/) and documentation support, along with duty and tax estimates when you provide shipment details.

Ask why a code applies to your product and whether it falls into a category prone to anti-dumping or countervailing duties. Even with forwarder support, you remain responsible for the accuracy of the classification and for paying the duties owed, so it pays to understand the reasoning, not just accept the number.

## A Named U.S. Customs Broker and Clear Power of Attorney Workflow

Ask for the name of the customs broker handling your entry and confirm what your Power of Attorney (POA) authorizes them to do. A POA gives your forwarder or broker legal authority to file customs entries and communicate with CBP on your behalf, and it’s required any time you want them to clear your shipment.

A dependable partner explains exactly what the POA covers and how long it stays active, in plain language, not buried in fine print. AEB Logistics requires a Power of Attorney when it performs customs clearance, and can arrange an annual bond for U.S. imports or offer use of its own bond on a case-by-case basis.

A customs bond is a financial guarantee to CBP that duties and fees will be paid; ask which bond type applies to your shipment and why. Confirm this in writing before your cargo departs China, since a vague answer here often signals a vague answer everywhere else.

## Verifiable FMC or NVOCC Credentials and Carrier Documentation

Verify that your forwarder holds an FMC license as an NVOCC (Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier) or works through properly licensed carrier partnerships for ocean freight. This licensing status affects who is legally accountable for your cargo if something goes wrong in transit, and it’s worth confirming before you book rather than after a problem surfaces.

Ask for the vessel booking confirmation once your container is booked. This document names the actual ocean carrier, the vessel, and the sailing schedule, which is different from a verbal promise about “a fast boat.”

For businesses registered in China, ask whether the forwarder’s legal entity is registered and verifiable through China’s National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System at [gsxt.gov.cn](https://www.gsxt.gov.cn). A forwarder willing to share registration details and carrier documentation without hesitation is showing you real operational standing, not just a sales pitch.

## Written Accessorial Fees and Shipment-Exception Escalation Procedures

Get accessorial fees and exception procedures in writing before your cargo ships, not after a surprise invoice arrives. Accessorial charges cover things like liftgate service, residential delivery, or extra handling at locations without a loading dock, and they should be listed as known possibilities in your original quote.

Ask what happens if your shipment misses a customs deadline, gets flagged for exam, or arrives at a port with congestion delays. A forwarder with a real escalation process can tell you who gets notified, how fast, and what your options are, rather than leaving you to discover the problem when your delivery doesn’t show up.

## How to Rank Freight Forwarders for Your Shipment

Ranking freight forwarders comes down to matching your shipment’s size and timeline to the right mode, then verifying the paperwork and pricing behind each quote. A forwarder that looks great on price but weak on documentation can cost you more in delays and customs holds than you save on freight.

### Which China-to-USA shipping mode fits your volume, budget, and deadline?

Your shipment size and deadline should drive the mode you choose, not the other way around. Air freight suits urgent or high-value cargo, with standard air freight running roughly 8 to 10 days and express courier options landing in about 3 to 5 days.

Ocean freight costs less for larger or heavier cargo but takes longer, with door-to-door ocean transit typically running 30 to 40 days. Full Container Load (FCL) fits larger shipments needing a dedicated 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container, while Less than Container Load (LCL) works for smaller volumes that don’t justify a full container.

### What evidence should a forwarder provide before you book?

Ask for documented proof, not verbal reassurance, before you commit cargo to any forwarder. That includes a written routing plan naming the origin and destination ports, a vessel booking confirmation once issued, ISF filing confirmation, and a reviewed commercial invoice and packing list that match your actual cargo.

A forwarder who produces these records on request, before you’ve paid a deposit, is showing real operational control. One who answers with vague reassurance is asking you to trust them blind.

### How should you compare quotes without overlooking landed-cost risks?

Compare quotes line by line, not by the bottom number alone. Request that each forwarder itemize ocean or air freight, customs brokerage (often around $125), drayage, and accessorial charges like the $100 liftgate fee separately.

### Which red flags suggest a forwarder may create customs or delivery problems?

Vague answers about ISF timing, reluctance to name the ocean carrier or vessel, and quotes with no itemized breakdown are the clearest warning signs. A forwarder who can’t explain your HS/HTS classification logic or who treats the Power of Attorney as a formality to sign without explanation deserves extra scrutiny.

Watch for forwarders unwilling to share registration details or carrier documentation when asked directly. Silence or evasiveness on these basic questions during the quote stage tends to predict the same behavior once your cargo is already in transit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I look for in a China to USA freight forwarder besides price?

Look for documented proof of operational readiness: written routing plans, ISF filing confirmation, HS/HTS classification support, and an itemized quote with no bundled surprises. A dedicated point of contact who tracks your shipment through clear milestones also matters more over time than a slightly lower headline rate.

### How do I verify that a freight forwarder can support U.S. customs clearance?

Ask for the name of the customs broker handling your entry and what your Power of Attorney authorizes. Confirm the bond type they use for your shipment, whether a single-entry bond, an annual bond, or use of the forwarder’s own bond, and get this in writing before cargo departs China.

### What charges should be included in a China to USA freight quote?

A complete quote separates ocean or air freight, customs brokerage (often around $125), drayage, documentation fees, and accessorial charges like liftgate service (commonly around $100) into distinct line items. Residential delivery and pickup fees should also be disclosed upfront rather than added to the final invoice.

### Does a freight forwarder need to file an ISF for ocean shipments to the USA?

Yes, ISF filing is required for ocean shipments and must reach U.S. Customs and Border Protection at least 24 hours before your cargo loads onto the vessel in China. A dependable forwarder requests your ISF data early and confirms the filing was accepted before departure.

### Can a freight forwarder deliver shipments directly to Amazon FBA warehouses?

Yes, many forwarders coordinate FBA delivery, including labeling, inventory splitting across multiple fulfillment centers, and scheduling through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal. Confirm whether the forwarder handles this directly or requires your own carrier’s bill of lading and FBA booking form to coordinate the appointment.

### How long does sea freight from China to the USA usually take?

Door-to-door ocean freight from China to the USA generally takes 30 to 40 days, though customs processing, port congestion, and seasonal demand can extend that window. Air freight moves faster, with standard service running about 8 to 10 days and express courier options taking roughly 3 to 5 days.
