## Core Checks Before You Compare Freight Forwarders

### Does The Quote Match Your Shipping Mode, Cargo Profile, And Incoterms?

A quote only means something after you confirm that it matches your actual shipping mode, cargo details, and Incoterms. Ocean FCL (full container load), ocean LCL (less than container load), air freight, and express courier all use different pricing models. Comparing a port-to-port ocean quote with a door-to-door air quote tells you nothing useful.

Give every forwarder the same inputs: box count, cubic meters, kilograms, HS code, cargo value, and whether you’re shipping under FOB, CIF, or EXW terms. Mismatched inputs create quotes that look comparable but aren’t.

Ask for the scope in writing. A quote that skips origin pickup or destination delivery may look cheaper simply because it covers less ground than the one beside it.

### Who Is Responsible For U.S. Customs Clearance, ISF Filing, And Customs Bonds?

Get a clear, written answer about who files your entry, submits the Importer Security Filing, and arranges your customs bond. Check whether they work with an experienced customs broker. For ocean shipments, the ISF, sometimes called 10+2 or ISF 10+2, must reach US Customs and Border Protection and CBP at least 24 hours before your container loads in China.

A customs bond guarantees payment of duties and fees to CBP, and shipments without one can face entry problems. Ask whether the forwarder can arrange an annual bond for repeat shipments or offer use of its own bond case-by-case. AEB Logistics does this depending on the shipment.

You also need a Power of Attorney whenever you ask a forwarder to handle customs clearance for you. Confirm what authority the document grants and how long it lasts, since some POAs cover one shipment while others remain active indefinitely.

If your products require partner government agency oversight, check whether they manage clearance for the FDA or FCC. Overlooking these filings can quickly bring your cargo to a standstill.

### Is The Importer Of Record Confirmed Before The Shipment Leaves China?

The Importer of Record carries legal responsibility for duties, classification, and entry accuracy. In nearly every case, that’s your business, not the forwarder. Your supplier in China cannot serve as the U.S. Importer of Record for a standard commercial import.

This includes ensuring your commercial invoice aligns with the Harmonized Tariff Schedule. Correct classifications determine your baseline import duties as well as any applicable Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods.

Confirm this weeks before departure, not after your container reaches port. If a forwarder stays vague about who holds the responsibility, treat that as a warning sign rather than something to sort out later.

For Amazon FBA sellers, this point carries extra weight. Amazon does not act as Importer of Record and won’t accept shipments with unpaid duties, taxes, or shipping charges.

### Are Duties, Taxes, Destination Charges, And Accessorial Fees Clearly Separated?

Ask for an itemized breakdown that separates freight, customs brokerage, duties, and delivery charges instead of accepting one bundled number. Duties and taxes remain the importer’s responsibility, regardless of what the forwarder’s invoice covers.

Accessorial fees catch many first-time importers off guard. Liftgate service at a location without a loading dock can cost an extra $100, while residential deliveries or pickups may bring additional charges.

Port delays can also trigger container drayage surcharges and daily storage fees. Knowing these potential costs in advance protects your margin from unexpected surprises.

Ask specifically whether customs brokerage comes bundled into your quote or gets billed separately. It’s a straightforward question, and a forwarder that provides an itemized breakdown on request shows the kind of transparency you should expect from every quote.

### Does The Forwarder Provide A Clear Escalation Path When Cargo Is Delayed?

You need to know whom to contact when something goes wrong, not scramble to find out after your cargo is already held. Ask what happens if CBP flags your shipment for inspection and how quickly the forwarder will notify you.

A dedicated account manager usually handles this better than a rotating support queue because one person already knows your shipment history and documentation. Ask how the forwarder tracks release status after cargo clears and whether updates come proactively or only when you call.

Real-time tracking with clear milestones, such as Import Entry, Import Arrival, and Delivered, gives you enough visibility to plan inventory instead of guessing.

## E-Commerce Requirements That Change The Right Choice

### Can The Forwarder Manage Amazon FBA Labels, Delivery Appointments, And Warehouse Splits?

Amazon FBA shipping adds requirements that general freight forwarding doesn’t cover, so confirm labeling and appointment handling before you book. This includes FBA labeling, UPS labeling where needed, and product inspection support before cartons ship.

Delivery appointments go through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal. For LCL shipments, scheduling typically happens after the goods arrive at the forwarder’s destination warehouse.

If you use your own carrier, the forwarder should still coordinate the appointment when you provide your bill of lading, Amazon FBA booking form, and carrier manual. Ask how shipment splitting works when Amazon divides one shipment across multiple fulfillment centers.

This is a common failure point as order volume grows. Inventory management support for replenishment cycles also becomes more valuable as your shipping frequency increases.

### What Does DDP Shipping Cover, And What Responsibilities Still Belong To The Importer?

DDP, or Delivered Duty Paid, means the forwarder handles duties, taxes, and clearance, so you receive one all-in number. Even under DDP, you remain the Importer of Record. Your business still carries legal responsibility for compliance, classification, and valuation.

Compare DDP with EXW and FOB carefully because each term shifts pickup responsibility and origin charges differently. EXW puts the most work on you, including pickup, export clearance, and freight. FOB shifts responsibility to the seller up to the origin port.

Ask exactly what your DDP quote includes and confirm that it matches [door-to-door shipping](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-shipping-from-china-to-usa/) in scope, not just in name.

### When Do Warehousing, Transload, Palletization, And LTL Delivery Become Necessary?

Warehousing becomes necessary when your shipment needs storage, consolidation, or splitting before final delivery. Services such as pick-and-pack, labeling, palletization, and transload, which moves cargo from an ocean container into a domestic truck, support both retail replenishment and FBA delivery.

Third-party warehouse arrangements can provide stable storage rates and a local ship-from address. That matters if you want to hold inventory outside Amazon and replenish fulfillment centers over time. Ask where the warehouse is located and whether the forwarder operates it directly or leases it.

LTL, or less-than-truckload delivery, fits final-mile moves that don’t need a full trailer. Confirm which carriers the forwarder uses for this leg, since inland delivery quality affects how reliably cargo reaches its destination on schedule.

### How Should You Compare Air, LCL, FCL, And Express Transit Times?

Match transit-time expectations to your cargo-ready date instead of assuming every mode moves at the same pace. Air freight from Hong Kong to Los Angeles can take around five days, while ocean freight on the same route can take at least three weeks.

General guidance puts standard air freight at around 8 to 10 days and express courier service at about 3 to 5 days. Door-to-door ocean freight takes closer to 30 to 40 days once you factor in customs processing and inland delivery.

Actual timing changes with port congestion, carrier schedules, and seasonal demand. FCL suits larger volumes or cargo that needs dedicated container space, while LCL fits shipments too small to fill a container alone.

### What Shipment Updates And Account-Manager Support Will You Receive?

You should see where your cargo sits at every stage, not just receive a tracking number that you have to interpret yourself. Milestones such as Accepted, In Transit, Export Customs, Export Departure, Import Entry, Import Arrival, and Delivered give you a useful read on progress.

Ask whether updates come through a dashboard, an account manager, or both. A single point of contact who knows your shipment history saves time compared with explaining the situation from scratch every time you call.

This matters most during a customs hold or delayed FBA appointment, when timely updates affect how quickly you can plan inventory.

## How To Request A Comparable China-to-USA Freight Quote

### Which Cargo Details Must Be Final Before You Request Pricing?

Lock in your box count, cubic meters, kilograms, cargo-ready date, and full commodity description before requesting quotes. A quote built on rough estimates will change once the forwarder sees your actual packing list, and that change can throw off your landed-cost calculations.

List every commodity type instead of using a vague category. Customs officials and forwarders both need this detail to classify goods correctly. Include your HS code if you already have one, or ask the forwarder to help confirm it.

Accurate addresses, weights, dimensions, and product descriptions prevent quote discrepancies and unexpected costs later.

### How Do EXW And FOB Terms Change Pickup And Origin Charges?

EXW and FOB determine where your costs begin, so confirm which term applies before comparing prices. Under EXW, you’re responsible for factory pickup, export clearance, and everything after, which often makes the freight rate look lower.

FOB shifts pickup and export handling to the seller up to the origin port. After that, freight and customs become your responsibility. Neither term is inherently better, but comparing an EXW quote with an FOB quote without adjusting for scope makes one look artificially cheaper.

Confirm which Incoterm each quote assumes and ask the forwarder to show origin charges separately from freight and destination costs.

### What Should An All-In Door-to-Door Quote Include?

A complete door-to-door quote should include factory pickup, export documentation, main transport, customs clearance coordination, destination handling, and final delivery. Anything less is a partial quote, even if the forwarder labels it door-to-door.

Ask for line items covering customs brokerage, often around $125 depending on commodity count, any bond arrangement, and accessorial charges such as liftgate service. Confirm whether cargo insurance is included or available as an add-on, particularly for FCL shipments carrying higher-value cargo.

Request the quote in writing with each component listed separately, not as one bundled figure.

### How Can You Compare Two Quotes Without Choosing On Price Alone?

Compare quotes on scope first, then price. Two quotes with similar prices can cover very different amounts of work, so match each proposal against the same checklist: Importer of Record confirmation, ISF filing responsibility, bond availability, FBA appointment coordination if relevant, and a full breakdown of destination charges.

Ask each forwarder for the same shipment details so you aren’t comparing an EXW ocean quote with an FOB air quote. A [side-by-side comparison](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-9-quote-checks-before-you-ship/) only works when the inputs match.

The forwarder willing to put responsibility assignments in writing, rather than answering verbally, is usually the safer choice once your inventory and capital are on the line.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I check before choosing a China-to-USA freight forwarder?

Check who handles customs clearance, ISF filing, and your customs bond, and get those answers in writing before booking. Confirm the Importer of Record and review the quote scope against your Incoterms.

### What is the difference between DDP shipping and door-to-door shipping from China?

DDP, or Delivered Duty Paid, means the forwarder collects duties and taxes as part of one all-in price. Door-to-door describes the physical route from the factory to the final destination, regardless of who pays the duties.

A shipment can be door-to-door without being DDP if you still handle duty payments separately as the importer.

### How do I choose a freight forwarder for Amazon FBA shipping?

Look for FBA labeling support, delivery appointment scheduling through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal, and shipment splitting across multiple fulfillment centers. Confirm whether the forwarder schedules appointments after LCL cargo arrives at its warehouse.

It’s also worth asking how the forwarder handles inventory replenishment for repeat shipments.

### How long does customs clearance take for shipments from China to the USA?

Customs clearance timing depends on documentation accuracy, HS code classification, and whether CBP flags a shipment for inspection. Door-to-door ocean freight from China to the USA generally takes 30 to 40 days in total.

Accurate paperwork and a confirmed Importer of Record can reduce the chance of holds that add extra time.

### What details do I need for an accurate China-to-USA freight quote?

You’ll need the origin and destination, FOB or EXW terms, HS code, cargo value, box count, cubic meters, kilograms, and your estimated cargo-ready date. Share these details upfront to avoid quote discrepancies and compare proposals from different forwarders on equal footing.
