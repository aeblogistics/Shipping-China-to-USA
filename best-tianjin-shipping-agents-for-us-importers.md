## How to Choose a Forwarder for Your Shipment

### Should you request FCL, LCL, or air freight?

Choose FCL when your cargo volume justifies a dedicated 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container. It reduces handling and keeps goods sealed from Tianjin to the destination port.

LCL suits smaller shipments when you’d rather pay only for the container space your cargo occupies. Meanwhile, air freight from China works for urgent or high-value orders where transit speed justifies the higher cost per kilogram.

### What changes when shipping to Amazon FBA?

Amazon FBA shipments require labeling, inspection, and delivery appointments, steps that general freight quotes don’t always cover. Sophisticated Amazon FBA logistics also involves palletization and prep work to avoid warehouse rejections.

AEB Logistics schedules appointments through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal and splits shipments across multiple Amazon warehouses when needed. That can matter for sellers managing inventory across several fulfillment centers.

Confirm whether your forwarder handles FBA labeling and appointment scheduling, or whether you’ll need to take care of those steps after the cargo reaches a US warehouse.

### Who will own shipment updates and resolve delivery problems?

Pin down a single point of contact before booking instead of assuming a general customer service line will track your shipment’s progress. A dedicated account manager can own your shipment from pickup through delivery and resolve documentation issues or delivery changes faster than a rotating support queue. AEB Logistics structures its service around that model.

### What cargo details should you send before requesting a quote?

Send the origin and destination, Incoterms (FOB or EXW), HS code or US HTS code, cargo value, box count, cubic meters, weight, and cargo-ready date.

Accurate details help prevent quote discrepancies after booking, since a forwarder can’t price freight accurately from a rough product description.
