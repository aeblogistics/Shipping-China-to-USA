Shipping products from China to the USA isn’t just about picking the cheapest quote. **The [forwarder you choose](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-from-china-to-the-usa/) affects your costs, delivery speed, and how smoothly your goods clear customs.**

If you sell on Amazon or run an e-commerce store, choosing the wrong provider can mean delays, extra fees, or even stockouts. It’s a headache nobody wants.

This article covers the [key questions](https://aeblogistics.com/10-factors-in-comparing-china-to-usa-forwarders/) to ask before signing a contract. You’ll learn what to check for when it comes to DDP shipping, customs clearance, and U.S. warehousing.

These questions should help you compare forwarders and find one that actually fits your business needs.

## Are you licensed and experienced with China–USA shipments?

Before picking a forwarder, check if they are [licensed to handle your shipments](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-how-to-choose-for-alibaba-imports/). In the U.S., forwarders should hold a Federal Maritime Commission (FMC) license for ocean freight.

If they also act as a customs broker, they need a license from U.S. Customs and Border Protection. Ask how long the company has [worked on China-to-USA routes](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-china-to-usa-best-china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-for-new-importers/).

A forwarder with years of experience will know how to avoid port delays and handle paperwork correctly. New or unlicensed forwarders could cost you time and money later.

Ask about their experience with your specific product type. Some goods, like electronics or food, need extra permits or inspections.

A forwarder who understands [customs clearance](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/how-to-choose-the-best-freight-forwarder-china-to-usa-a-complete-guide.html) rules for your industry can help you avoid fines or holds. [Request proof of licensing](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-in-china/) and ask for references from other importers.

A trustworthy forwarder will share this information without hesitation. If they dodge basic questions about credentials, that’s a red flag.

## Which shipping modes and routes do you offer?

Ask each forwarder which shipping modes they actually offer. Most provide [sea freight](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-services-comparing-china-usa-providers/), air freight, and express options, but not all handle every mode equally well.

Sea freight works best for large or heavy shipments where cost matters more than speed. Air freight is better for urgent or lighter goods, since it’s faster but pricier.

Express services fit small parcels, though they get expensive fast if you scale up. You also need to ask about [specific routes](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-9-quote-checks-before-you-ship/).

Find out which Chinese ports and airports they use, and which U.S. ports or airports they ship to. Some forwarders focus on the [Trans-Pacific route](https://www.globalsources.com/knowledge/shipping-routes-from-china-to-usa/), connecting major Chinese ports directly to the U.S. West Coast.

Ask if they offer both full container load (FCL) and less than container load (LCL) options for ocean freight. This matters if your shipment sizes change from order to order.

Can they combine modes, like air freight for urgent items and sea freight for the rest? A forwarder with [flexible options](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-10-capabilities-us-importers-should-evaluate/) and clear routing details gives you more control over cost and delivery time.

## What is included in your quoted price?

A low quote can be tempting. But what does it actually cover?

Ask your forwarder to break down every charge. This should include ocean or air freight, [origin and destination fees](https://seafreightgo.com/freight-quotation-explained-guide/), and handling charges at each port.

Check if [customs clearance, duties, and taxes](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-9-quote-checks-before-you-ship/) are included or billed separately. Many quotes leave these out, which can mean a bigger bill later.

Ask about surcharges. Fuel costs, peak season fees, and documentation fees often pop up if they’re not listed upfront.

If you’re comparing quotes, make sure you’re comparing the same services. One quote might include [door-to-door delivery](https://aeblogistics.com/door-to-door-shipping-quotes-china-to-usa/), while another only covers port-to-port shipping.

Get everything in writing. A clear, itemized quote protects you from surprise fees and helps you [compare offers fairly](https://www.freightmango.com/blog/understand-freight-quotation-tips-ensure-accurate-quote/).

## Are customs clearance fees, duties, and taxes included?

Ask your forwarder exactly what is included in the quote. Some quotes only cover [freight from China to the U.S.](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-the-usa/) port or airport, not the final steps.

A [customs clearance fee](https://chinaoceanlogistics.com/customs-clearance-fee-what-does-customs-clearance-really-cost-importing-to-usa/) pays for the broker’s work to get your goods released by U.S. Customs and Border Protection. This fee doesn’t automatically cover duties, taxes, or exam charges.

Find out if import duties and [Section 301 tariffs](https://www.tonlexing.com/china-to-usa-import-duties/) are part of your total cost or billed separately. These charges depend on your product’s classification, so ask your forwarder to confirm the rate that applies to you.

If you choose [DDP shipping](https://presou.com/china-to-usa-ddp-shipping-taxes-duties-included-2026/), duties and taxes are usually built into one price. This option gives you more certainty over your total landed cost.

Also ask about the Merchandise Processing Fee and Harbor Maintenance Fee. These smaller charges add up, and you want them listed upfront instead of appearing as surprises on your [final invoice](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-the-usa-2/).

Get a [written breakdown](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-from-china-to-the-usa/) before you commit. A clear list of fees protects you from unexpected costs later.

## Will you provide DDP, DAP, or FOB shipping options?

Ask your forwarder which shipping terms they support before signing anything. Each option changes who handles customs, who pays duties, and where your risk ends.

With FOB, you take charge once goods leave the Chinese port. You’ll need your own customs broker in the U.S. and must handle duty payments yourself.

DAP shifts more work to the forwarder. They manage transport to your U.S. address, but you still handle [customs clearance](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-from-china-to-usa-essential-customs-strategies/) and duty payments on your end.

[DDP](https://aeblogistics.com/ddp-freight-forwarder-china-to-usa/) is the most hands-off option for you. The forwarder covers freight, customs clearance, and duties, so your goods arrive ready to use without extra steps.

A good forwarder will explain the [differences between DDP, DAP, and FOB](https://cargolinked.com/guides/ddp-vs-dap-vs-fob-delivery-terms-explained) clearly and help you pick the right one for your shipment size and experience level.

If you’re new to importing or ship small volumes, [DDP shipping](https://vantageforwarding.com/what-is-ddp-shipping/) often reduces surprises. Larger or more experienced importers may prefer FOB or DAP for more control over costs.

Confirm which terms your forwarder offers and whether they can switch between them as your business grows. Flexibility here can make a big difference.

## How do you calculate dimensional and chargeable weight?

Freight forwarders bill you based on [chargeable weight](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china-to-us/), not just the actual weight of your box. Chargeable weight is [the greater of your actual weight and your volumetric weight](https://everycalculators.com/calculation-of-chargeable-weight.html).

To find volumetric weight, multiply the length, width, and height of your package, then divide by a dimensional factor set by your carrier. Air freight typically uses a divisor of 6,000, while courier shipments often use 5,000.

Ocean freight is a bit different. Forwarders usually use CBM (cubic meters) to calculate volume, then [compare that against gross weight](https://shipcalculators.com/wiki/cbm-and-chargeable-weight) to set the chargeable rate.

Before choosing a forwarder, ask how they calculate chargeable weight for your specific shipments. Different carriers use different divisors, and that affects your final cost.

Large, lightweight items often cost more to ship than small, heavy ones because their dimensional weight exceeds their actual weight. Ask your forwarder to walk you through a [sample calculation](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-for-smbs/) using your product dimensions.

This helps you [avoid surprise charges](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-quote-target-price-benefits-budgeting-comparison-customization-negotiation-satisfactory-solution/) and [compare quotes accurately](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-quote/) across different companies.

## What are the estimated transit times and cutoff dates?

[Transit times vary a lot](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-shipping-cost-and-time/) depending on [shipping method and route](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-shipping-from-china-to-usa/). [Ask your forwarder](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-china-to-usa-2/) for real numbers, not vague promises.

For sea freight, expect [35 to 75 days to the West Coast and 50 to 90 days to the East Coast](https://plainfreight.com/guides/china-to-usa-shipping-timeline), counting from factory to warehouse. If speed matters more, DDP ocean shipping can run [20 to 25 days](https://vantageforwarding.com/china-to-usa-freight-shipping-time-guide/).

[Sea freight overall ranges](https://aeblogistics.com/sea-freight-from-china-to-usa/) from [20 to 45 days](https://sinoshipment.com/blogs/how-long-does-shipping-take-from-china-to-usa/). Air freight and express options move faster.

You can expect [3 to 10 days by air and 2 to 7 days by express](https://sinoshipment.com/blogs/how-long-does-shipping-take-from-china-to-usa/), though costs are higher. Cutoff dates matter just as much as transit time.

Every carrier sets a deadline for booking cargo before a vessel or flight departs. Miss it, and your shipment waits for the next available slot.

Ask your forwarder for their weekly cutoff schedule. This helps you plan production timelines and avoid last-minute delays.

Also ask how they handle peak season congestion, customs holds, and port delays. A forwarder who gives clear, realistic timelines is more trustworthy than one who promises the fastest speed without explaining potential risks.

## How will I track my shipment from pickup to delivery?

A good forwarder should give you visibility at every stage of your shipment’s journey. You’ll want to see updates from the moment your [goods are picked up](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-freight-from-china/) in China until they land at your U.S. address or warehouse.

Ask your forwarder what [tracking tools](https://aeblogistics.com/can-i-track-my-shipment/) they offer. Many companies now have online portals or apps for real-time status updates.

Your shipment passes through several checkpoints. It goes from pickup to a consolidation point, through [China customs clearance](https://www.freightos.com/freight-resources/shipment-process/), onto a ship or plane, and then through U.S. customs before last-mile delivery.

Some forwarders only provide tracking numbers for certain legs of the trip. Others offer [end-to-end tracking across the full route](https://pigeepost.com/how-to-track-every-shipment-from-dispatch-to-delivery/), which is much more useful if you need to plan around possible delays.

Check if their tracking works for both air and sea freight. Sea freight tracking usually updates less often than air, so keep that in mind when choosing your method.

Find out if you’ll get alerts for delays, customs holds, or missed connections. Good communication at each step helps you manage inventory and avoid stockouts.

## How China-to-U.S. Shipping Works

Getting goods from a factory in China to your warehouse or Amazon fulfillment center is complicated. You’ll need to know the freight modes, who takes on risk, and who handles customs paperwork before signing on with a forwarder.

### Freight Modes and Transit Expectations

Your main choices are ocean freight, air freight, and express courier services. Each one comes with different costs and speeds.

**[Ocean freight](https://aeblogistics.com/ocean-freight-china-to-usa/)** is the cheapest option for large shipments, but it’s also the slowest. You’ll be picking between:

- **[FCL (Full Container Load)](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-ocean-freight-case-study-end-to-end-logistics-execution-for-industrial-machinery/)**: You pay for the whole container, even if it’s not full. Transit time usually runs 20-35 days.
- **LCL (Less than Container Load)**: You share container space with others. This can save money but adds handling time at both ends.
 
**Air freight** cuts transit time down to 5-10 days. It costs a lot more per kilogram than ocean freight, though.

**Express services** like DHL, FedEx, or UPS are best for small parcels or samples. They do door-to-door delivery in 3-7 days, but the per-kilo price is the highest of all three options.

### Incoterms, Risk Transfer, and DDP Shipping

[Incoterms](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-glossary-china-to-usa-shipping-100-terms/) tell you when responsibility for your goods shifts from the seller to you. Two terms really matter for importers shipping from China.

**FOB (Free on Board)** means the seller’s responsibility ends as soon as the goods are loaded onto the ship at the Chinese port. After that, you own the cargo and handle everything—ocean freight, insurance, customs, and delivery.

**DDP ([Delivered Duty Paid](https://aeblogistics.com/ddp-shipping-china-to-usa/))** puts nearly all responsibility on the seller or forwarder. The forwarder arranges transport, pays import duties, clears customs, and delivers to your address. You pay one flat fee up front.

DDP is popular with Amazon FBA sellers because it removes the guesswork on customs and duty payments. Still, DDP rates usually include a margin for the forwarder’s extra risk and paperwork, so [compare it](https://aeblogistics.com/best-freight-forwarder-china-to-usa/) to FOB plus your own customs costs before you pick one.

### Customs Clearance Responsibilities

U.S. Customs and Border Protection asks for specific documents before your goods can enter the country. You’ll need a commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and sometimes an ISF (Importer Security Filing) submitted 24 hours before the vessel leaves China.

If you import under **FOB terms**, you or your customs broker file these documents and pay duties directly. With **DDP shipping**, your forwarder or their broker handles this process for you.

If you misclassify products under the wrong HTS (Harmonized Tariff Schedule) code, you can run into [tariff stack complications and compliance issues](https://artemusgroupusa.com/shipping-from-china-to-usa/) that delay your shipment or trigger penalties. Ask your forwarder how they handle product classification and who’s responsible for mistakes.

## Preparing for Reliable Fulfillment

Getting your products from a [factory in China](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-shanghai-to-usa/) to a [customer’s doorstep in the U.S.](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-from-china-to-the-usa/) takes more than just picking a shipping method. You have to plan for FBA rules, [warehouse space](https://aeblogistics.com/warehousing/), and paperwork before your goods even leave the port.

### Amazon FBA Logistics Requirements

Amazon enforces strict rules for how goods arrive at its [fulfillment centers](https://aeblogistics.com/fba-fulfillment-by-amazon/). Your forwarder needs to know these rules and follow them closely, or your [shipment could be rejected](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarding-red-flags-7-warning-signs-to-watch-for-in-china-to-usa-shipping/).

Boxes must meet Amazon’s size and weight limits, and labels need to be placed in the right spot on each box. Your forwarder should also know how to split shipments across multiple fulfillment centers, since Amazon often requires this.

Some forwarders offer FBA prep services. This includes labeling, poly-bagging, and bundling items before they get to the warehouse.

Ask if your forwarder handles this in-house or outsources it, since that affects both cost and quality control. A good forwarder will keep up with Amazon’s [FBA logistics requirements](https://winsaillogistics.com/top-freight-forwarders-for-usa-imports-from-china/) as they change, because Amazon updates policies all the time.

### U.S. Warehousing and Inventory Distribution

Storing goods in the U.S. before they reach your customer saves time and money. A forwarder with U.S. warehouse space can hold your inventory and ship out smaller batches as needed.

This setup is handy if you sell on multiple platforms, not just Amazon. You can keep bulk inventory in one place and ship small orders to different marketplaces or directly to customers.

Ask your forwarder these questions about warehousing:

- Where are their warehouse locations?
- Do they charge monthly storage fees or per-pallet fees?
- Can they handle returns and reverse logistics?
- Do they offer real-time inventory tracking?
 
Warehousing close to major ports or population centers can cut delivery times. That matters if you promise fast shipping to your customers.

### Documentation and Compliance Planning

Customs paperwork is a common reason shipments get delayed. Missing or incorrect documents can hold your goods at the border for days or even weeks.

Your forwarder should handle key documents like the commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading. They also need to know your product’s HTS code, since that determines the duty rate you’ll pay.

DDP shipping means your forwarder manages [customs clearance](https://aideliv.com/ddp-shipping-from-china) and duty payment for you. This can make things easier, but you should still understand which fees are included and which ones might pop up later.

Ask your forwarder how they handle compliance for products with special rules—think electronics, batteries, or anything under FDA review. A forwarder who knows your product category can help you avoid expensive mistakes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

These questions cover the practical details you’ll want to check before signing a contract, like service scope, pricing, customs handling, FBA delivery rules, and warehousing support.

### What services should a forwarder provide for shipments from China to the United States?

A qualified forwarder should handle booking, documentation, customs clearance, and delivery to your final destination. This includes ocean freight, [air freight](https://aeblogistics.com/freight-forwarder-ningbo-to-usa/), or a mix of both, depending on your timeline and budget.

Ask if they manage licensing, insurance, and cargo tracking as standard. You want a [single point of contact](https://aeblogistics.com/top-qualities-of-leading-forwarders/) who can answer questions about your shipment [from booking to delivery](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-2026-comparison-costs-and-best-services/).

Some forwarders only handle port-to-port service and leave you to arrange customs brokerage and inland trucking. Confirm exactly where their responsibility starts and ends before you commit.

### How can I compare freight quotes and identify hidden fees before booking?

Request an itemized quote that breaks down ocean or air freight, customs clearance, duties, inland trucking, and any handling fees. A low base rate can hide extra charges that show up later on your invoice.

Ask whether fuel surcharges, peak season fees, or warehouse storage charges apply. Comparing [quotes side by side](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa-freight-forwarders-in-depth-2026-comparison-guide/) helps you spot forwarders who bundle costs versus those who tack on fees after the fact.

Get quotes in writing, not just verbal estimates. This gives you a record to check against your final bill.

### Should I choose DDP shipping, and who serves as the importer of record?

DDP, or Delivered Duty Paid, means the forwarder handles duties, taxes, and customs clearance for you. This can make your import process easier, especially if you’re new to shipping from China.

Under DDP terms, you still need to confirm who is listed as the [importer of record](https://aeblogistics.com/ddp-shipping-risk-when-shipping-from-china/). Often, the forwarder or a customs broker takes this role, but you should get that in writing.

Compare DDP against DAP or FOB terms to see what fits your business model. Each option shifts different responsibilities and costs between you and the forwarder [depending on the shipping terms you choose](https://aideliv.com/ddp-shipping-from-china).

### How does a forwarder handle U.S. customs clearance and duty payments?

Ask how the forwarder submits entry documents to U.S. Customs and Border Protection. They should explain their process for filing your import declaration and figuring out duties owed.

Confirm if duties and taxes are included in your quote or billed separately after clearance. Some forwarders pay duties up front and invoice you later, while others want payment before your goods clear customs.

Ask about their experience with your specific product category, since some goods need extra permits or are subject to [Section 301 tariffs](https://www.unicargo.com/how-to-import-from-china-to-usa-2026/). A forwarder who knows your HS code can help you avoid delays at the border.

### What experience should a provider have with Amazon FBA deliveries and appointment requirements?

[Amazon FBA shipments](https://aeblogistics.com/shipping-to-amazon-fba/) come with strict delivery appointment windows at fulfillment centers. You’ll want to ask the forwarder how many FBA shipments they handle each month.

Find out if they track appointment slots directly. That detail can make a big difference in avoiding missed windows and headaches later.

It’s also smart to check if they really get Amazon’s labeling rules and carton marking. Missing those can lead to chargebacks, and nobody wants that.

Does your forwarder know how to split shipments to different fulfillment centers if Amazon asks for it? That’s something only experienced providers can handle smoothly.

Ask for references from other Amazon sellers they work with right now. Hearing from other sellers gives you a clearer sense of what to expect with FBA logistics.

### Can the forwarder offer U.S. warehousing, inspection, labeling, and fulfillment support?

Plenty of importers want more than just delivery to one address. It’s worth asking if the forwarder runs or works with U.S. warehouses for storage, inspection, or repackaging.

Find out if they can apply FBA labels, check product quality, or handle returns. This really matters if you sell on more than just Amazon, since you might need flexible fulfillment options.

Where are their warehouses, exactly? Ask how they calculate storage fees, too.

Location changes your shipping times, and fee structures can be all over the place—some use a flat rate, others charge by usage.
