## Choose Your Shanghai Ocean Freight Service

**Your cargo volume, not your budget alone, should decide between FCL and LCL ocean freight from Shanghai.** Businesses moving enough goods to fill a full container pay less per unit with FCL. Businesses with smaller volumes save money with LCL consolidation, even if the per-unit math looks less favorable upfront.

Both options start from the same place: a Chinese port near your supplier, where cargo gets loaded onto vessels bound for US ports. Customs clearance and inland delivery happen at the other end.

### Full Container Load for Dedicated Container Space

FCL gives your shipment exclusive use of a sealed container. Importers can choose standard 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube container equipment depending on volume.

A 40-foot high-cube container provides extra vertical capacity, making it ideal for voluminous or palletized cargo. No other cargo shares the container, which reduces handling and transit damage.

This option makes sense once your shipment fills roughly 10 to 13 cubic meters or more. Below that threshold, you’re usually paying for space you don’t use.

For manufacturers and importers moving steady, larger volumes from Shanghai, our [ocean freight china to usa](https://aeblogistics.com/ocean-freight-china-to-usa/) service explains how container options match shipment size.

### Less than Container Load and Multi-Supplier Consolidation

LCL shipping lets smaller shipments share container space with other importers’ cargo, so you pay only for the volume you use. It suits businesses still building order volume, or those testing a new product line before committing to full containers.

LCL consolidation also helps buyers sourcing from multiple Shanghai-area suppliers. Combining shipments into one container means one bill of lading instead of several, which simplifies paperwork and often lowers the total freight cost. Our guide to [shanghai to usa shipping with supplier consolidation](https://aeblogistics.com/china-to-usa/shanghai-to-usa-shipping-with-supplier-consolidation/) explains how the process works in practice.

### Which US Gateway Fits Your Delivery Address?

Most Shanghai-origin containers ship to West Coast ports like Los Angeles and Long Beach, which generally have faster port-to-port transit than East Coast gateways. Cargo bound for New York/New Jersey, Savannah, or other East Coast ports typically takes longer, as vessels either transit the Panama Canal or transfer cargo onto rail partway through the US.

Houston, Seattle/Tacoma, and Oakland are additional gateway options, depending on your distribution footprint. The right port depends on where your warehouse or Amazon fulfillment center sits, not just which port offers the shortest ocean leg.

## How Much Will Your Shipment Cost?

**Your total cost depends on chargeable weight, container type, and how much of the move your quote covers, not a single flat rate.** A 40-foot container, a shared LCL pallet, and a fully quoted door-to-door shipment each price out differently. Comparing line items matters more than comparing one headline number.

### What Determines Your Ocean Freight Quote?

Base ocean freight rates are priced by container (FCL) or by cubic meter (LCL), and both shift with seasonal demand, carrier capacity, and fuel surcharges. A quote based on your cargo-ready date, HS code, and cubic meters will be more accurate than one built on rough estimates.

Major ocean carriers like Maersk adjust sailings and capacity based on weekly transpacific volume. Knowing these schedule shifts helps you secure favorable terms when planning shipping from China to USA ports.

### Compare Origin, Destination, and Final-Delivery Charges

A freight rate alone doesn’t tell you what you’ll actually pay. Terminal handling charges, destination drayage, and documentation fees add to the base ocean freight rate. If a quote leaves these out, it isn’t showing the real total landed cost.

When doing a freight forwarder comparison, ask each provider to itemize origin charges, ocean freight, destination charges, and delivery separately.

### How FOB, EXW, and DDP Change the Quoted Scope

Incoterms determine where your responsibility, and cost, starts. Under FOB, your supplier handles origin charges up to the port. Under EXW, you’re responsible for pickup from the factory door. With DDP shipping, duties and taxes are settled before the cargo arrives, so there’s no separate customs bill waiting at delivery.

Confirm which Incoterm applies to your quote before booking. It changes what’s already paid for and what shows up later.

### Budget for Customs Brokerage, Bonds, and Insurance

Customs brokerage can be included in your shipment quote and typically runs around USD 125, depending on the number of commodities involved. For US imports, we can arrange an annual US customs bond or offer the use of an AEB bond on a case-by-case basis. Cargo insurance pricing depends on declared value, so higher-value shipments carry a higher premium.
