### How Weight, CBM, and Delivery Conditions Affect Shipping Costs

Freight rates use chargeable weight, which is whichever is greater between actual weight and volumetric weight. For ocean freight, cost is based on cubic meters (CBM). For air freight, carriers compare gross weight with dimensional weight and charge the higher figure.

Fuel surcharges, destination charges, and cargo value, which affects insurance and customs valuation, also factor into your final landed cost. Adding cargo insurance gives you extra financial protection against unexpected transit risks.

### DDP vs. DAP: Who Pays Duties and Taxes?

DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) means duties and taxes are paid before your cargo arrives, so there’s no separate bill at delivery. DAP (Delivered at Place) means the seller or forwarder handles transport and delivery, but you pay import duties and taxes directly.

Many importers choose DDP shipping because it bundles transport, customs clearance, and tariffs into one predictable cost. Importers remain legally responsible for duties and taxes regardless of the Incoterm used.

For Amazon FBA shipments, Amazon does not act as Importer of Record and will reject shipments with unpaid duties or shipping charges.

## Which China-USA Shipping Method Fits Your Cargo and Timeline?

Choosing the right method for shipping from China to USA depends on your cargo’s weight, urgency, and budget. The right shipping method from China to the USA balances cost against delivery speed.

Express courier is fastest for small urgent parcels, while ocean freight usually costs less for bulk cargo.

Most importers land somewhere between these options, depending on what they’re shipping and how soon they need it.

### Express Courier for Samples and Urgent Shipments

Express courier services like DHL, UPS, and FedEx work well for samples, replacement parts, or small urgent shipments. When speed is your top priority, express shipping ensures cargo arrives in about 3-5 days.

These carriers handle pickup, customs, and delivery as one bundled service. Express pricing runs highest per kilogram of any method, so it fits small shipments better than full inventory restocks.

### Air Freight for Time-Sensitive Commercial Cargo

Air freight suits commercial shipments where speed matters more than per-unit cost. An air shipment from Hong Kong to Los Angeles can take around five days, while general guidance across the industry puts standard air freight closer to 7-10 days, depending on the route and destination.

Ocean freight for the same route can take at least three weeks. That’s why air freight makes sense for new product launches or urgent replenishment orders.

### LCL Sea Freight for Smaller Shipments

LCL (Less than Container Load) lets you ship without filling a full container. AEB Logistics consolidates your cargo into a shared container with other shippers, so you pay only for the space you use.

LCL works well for smaller volumes and includes door-to-door transportation, customs clearance coordination, and delivery to Amazon FBA.

### FCL Ocean Freight for Heavy and Bulk Cargo

FCL (Full Container Load) fits larger shipments or cargo needing extra security, using 20-foot, 40-foot, or 40-foot high-cube containers. Booking FCL sea freight gives you a dedicated container that stays sealed from pickup through delivery, which usually means fewer handling touchpoints.

FCL can offer a lower per-unit shipping cost for businesses moving higher volumes regularly.

### DDP Air Freight and DDP Sea Freight Options

Both air and sea freight can be arranged under DDP terms, where duties and taxes get settled before your cargo arrives at its U.S. destination. Selecting air freight DDP delivers critical inventory quickly, whereas sea freight DDP offers significant savings on large shipments.

## How the Shipping Process and Customs Clearance Work

Customs clearance for China-to-USA shipments happens in two stages: export clearance in China before departure, and import clearance in the USA before delivery. Missing paperwork at either stage can hold up your shipment for days, so preparation matters more than reacting after cargo has already left the factory.

### Documents Needed Before Cargo Is Ready

Before your goods leave the supplier’s warehouse, you need a commercial invoice, a packing list, and accurate carton dimensions. Preparing compliant customs documentation early ensures a smooth journey through international border checkpoints.

The HS code on your commercial invoice determines your duty rate, so getting it right from the start avoids costly reclassification later.

### Export Customs in China and International Transport

Export customs clearance happens at the Chinese port or airport before cargo departs. Chinese customs officials review your export documents.

Your freight forwarder then issues a bill of lading for ocean shipments or an air waybill for air cargo. This carrier document, commonly called an airway bill in air transport, serves as your proof of shipment.

### U.S. Customs Entry, Duties, and Release

Once cargo arrives at a U.S. port or airport, import customs clearance begins. A customs broker submits your entry documents to U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), which calculates duties, taxes, and processing fees based on your declared value and HS code.

Clear filings with US Customs and Border Protection keep freight moving without port holds. The importer of record remains responsible for these charges.

A Power of Attorney is required when AEB Logistics handles customs clearance on your behalf. We can arrange an annual bond or offer use of an AEB bond for U.S. imports on a case-by-case basis.

### Delivery Scheduling, Tracking, and Receipt

After customs release, inland trucking carries your cargo to its final destination. You can track shipment milestones including Accepted, In Transit, Export Customs, Export Departure, Import Entry, Import Arrival, and Delivered.

For Amazon FBA orders, we schedule delivery appointments through Amazon’s portal once cargo clears, or after LCL freight arrives at our destination-country warehouse.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is included in door-to-door shipping from China to the USA?

Door-to-door shipping typically includes supplier pickup, export handling, international transport, import customs coordination, and final delivery to your address. What’s actually covered in a specific quote can vary, so confirm the scope in writing before your cargo ships, especially around customs duties and destination fees.

### How long does door-to-door shipping from China to the USA take?

Total shipping time depends largely on the transport method you choose. Express courier usually takes about 3-5 days, standard air freight about 8-10 days, and ocean freight door-to-door around 30-40 days.

Beyond standard transit time, customs processing, port congestion, and seasonal demand can extend your final arrival schedule.

### What is the cheapest way to ship from China to the USA?

Ocean freight, especially FCL for larger shipments, generally offers the lowest cost per unit when the cargo isn’t time-sensitive. For smaller shipments, LCL may cost less because you pay only for the space your cargo uses.

### Will I have to pay tariffs, duties, or taxes when importing from China?

Yes, importers remain responsible for duties and taxes regardless of the Incoterm. With DDP, the seller settles these costs before delivery as part of one price. With DAP, you pay them separately after the cargo clears U.S. customs.

### What information do I need for a China-to-USA shipping quote?

An accurate quote needs your origin and destination, Incoterms (FOB or EXW), HS code, cargo value, box count, cubic meters or kilograms, and estimated cargo-ready date. Accurate weights, dimensions, and product descriptions upfront help prevent quote discrepancies and unexpected costs later.

### Can AEB Logistics deliver shipments from China to Amazon FBA warehouses?

Yes, AEB Logistics is a member of the Amazon Service Provider Network. It supports FBA labeling, inspection, palletization, and delivery appointment scheduling, including shipment splitting when inventory needs to reach multiple Amazon fulfillment centers.
