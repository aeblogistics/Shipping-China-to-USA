Direct conversations with account managers do.

Oversized machinery, palletized hazmat, and regulated imports all carry operational requirements that a generic freight forwarder comparison won’t surface. You need to ask pointed questions and listen for vague answers.

## Can the Forwarder Handle Your Exact Dangerous-Goods Classification?

Ask for the specific UN number, not a general “yes, we ship batteries” answer. Lithium-ion cells, aerosols, and certain coatings each carry different packaging groups and documentation requirements under IMDG (ocean) and IATA (air) rules.

A forwarder who can name your product’s UN number, packing group, and required Material Safety Data Sheet without hesitation has done this before. One who says “we’ll figure it out at booking” is telling you they haven’t handled your commodity.

Ask specifically:

- Do they need a Dangerous Goods Declaration before booking space?
- Can they confirm which carriers accept your UN class on your lane?
- Do they charge a hazmat surcharge, and is it disclosed upfront?
 
Vague reassurance here is the biggest red flag in any China-USA logistics review. Get the answer in writing before you commit inventory to a shipping schedule.

## Will the Quote Itemize Origin, Freight, Customs, and Final-Mile Charges?

A usable quote separates every cost bucket by name. That means origin handling, ocean or air freight, customs clearance, import duties, and taxes (or a clear statement that they’re billed separately), and final-mile delivery each show up as their own line.

Clarify whether government fees like the merchandise processing fee and harbor maintenance fee are itemized or absorbed into customs fees. You should also confirm whether cargo insurance is included to protect against ocean damage.

Freight forwarding pricing that bundles everything into one number without a breakdown makes it impossible to compare providers fairly. It also hides where accessorial fees might land later, like liftgate charges or storage past a free period.

Ask the forwarder to walk through a sample invoice for a shipment similar to yours. If they can’t produce one, or the number changes when you ask for detail, treat that as a signal to keep shopping. ## Can They Move Oversized Crates, Machinery, or Non-Standard Pallets?

Oversized and heavy cargo needs equipment and documentation that standard parcel or FCL bookings don’t require. Ask whether the forwarder arranges specialized inland trucking, flatbed hauling, crane offload, or oversized permits on your specific U.S. delivery corridor.

Confirm how they calculate chargeable weight for irregular dimensions. Volumetric weight and dimensional weight rules can make a large, light crate cost more to ship than a small, heavy one. Ask for a sample calculation using your actual crate dimensions.

Also ask about:

- Whether their FCL container options (20-foot, 40-foot, 40-foot high-cube) fit your equipment’s footprint
- Access to flatbed or specialized trailers for delivery beyond the port
- Experience coordinating crane or forklift offload at non-dock sites
 
A forwarder without machinery experience will often quote standard LTL rates that don’t apply once your freight reaches the receiving site. ## What Customs-Entry Support Is Included for Regulated Imports?

Customs-entry support should cover code classification under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, import-entry documentation, and coordination with a licensed customs broker. Ask specifically whether the forwarder holds its own broker license or works with a partner broker, since these are legally distinct roles under U.S. Customs and Border Protection rules.

For ocean shipments, verify that they handle the Importer Security Filing on schedule. Ensure the forwarder reviews your commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading before loading to prevent customs holds.

If your product needs FDA registration, FCC certification, or a specific agency permit, ask whether the forwarder has handled that paperwork before or only general commercial entries. Regulated goods like electronics, food-contact items, or certain chemicals often get flagged for additional review at entry.

Confirm whether a Power of Attorney is required for them to file on your behalf, and ask how they estimate duties and Section 301 tariffs before the shipment lands. Get this confirmed in writing, since verbal assurances about classification don’t hold up if U.S. Customs disagrees with the HS code used. ## Who Pays for Customs Exams, Demurrage, Storage, and Other Exceptions?

You pay for exceptions unless the contract states otherwise, so get this in writing before booking. Customs exams, container demurrage, port storage, and chassis fees are common on regulated or high-value cargo, and they’re rarely included in a base quote.

Ask the forwarder directly: if U.S. Customs pulls your container for an intensive exam, who arranges it and who pays the exam fee? Ask the same about demurrage if a vessel is delayed or your customs entry takes longer than the free storage window allows.

A transparent forwarder will explain these as pass-through costs tied to actual carrier or port invoices, not padded markups. One who says “don’t worry about it” without specifics is leaving you exposed to a surprise bill weeks after your goods have shipped.

## Do They Offer the Right DDP, DAP, EXW, or FOB Service Scope?

Your Incoterm choice determines who handles customs and who carries risk at each leg. Under FOB, you take control once cargo clears the Chinese port and need your own U.S. customs broker. Under DAP, the forwarder manages transport to your address but you still handle customs and duties. Under DDP, the forwarder covers freight, customs, and duties in one price.

Ask which terms the forwarder supports and whether they can adjust as your shipment volume grows. New importers often start with DDP for cost certainty, then shift to FOB or EXW once they have their own broker relationships.

Confirm scope details:

- Does DDP pricing include duties and Section 301 tariffs, or are those separate?
- Can they switch Incoterms mid-relationship without renegotiating the whole contract?
- Do they explain, in plain terms, where their responsibility ends under each term?
 
Review the [differences between FOB, CIF, and DDP shipping terms](https://aeblogistics.com/top-10-differences-between-fob-vs-cif-vs-ddp-shipping-terms/) before you lock in a service scope.

## Can They Coordinate Supplier Pickup, Inspection, Labeling, and Consolidation?

Factory pickup and pre-shipment coordination determine how much manual work falls on you before goods even leave China. Ask whether the forwarder can arrange pickup directly from your Alibaba supplier or manufacturer, or whether you need to deliver cargo to a consolidation point yourself.

Ask about inspection support, since a forwarder who can arrange a third-party inspection before loading helps catch quality issues before they become a returns problem in the U.S. If your inventory is marketplace-bound, verify if they offer FBA prep services such as polybagging, bundling, and carton labeling.

For multi-supplier orders, ask specifically how they handle consolidated shipping:

- Do they combine cargo from different factories into one container?
- How do they track partial shipments if one supplier runs late?
- What’s their process for splitting a consolidated shipment across multiple delivery points?
 
A forwarder who handles these steps well reduces the coordination burden on your team significantly.

## Do Their U.S. Warehouse and Delivery Options Fit Your Receiving Site?

Ask what happens to your cargo the moment it lands, before it’s confirmed for final delivery. Warehouse and shipping services vary widely: some forwarders offer transload, pick-and-pack, and short-term storage, while others only hand off to a trucking partner with no buffer capacity.

Confirm whether their warehouse network can hold inventory if your receiving site isn’t ready, and whether pricing for that storage is disclosed upfront. Ask about delivery-site details too, since a facility without a loading dock typically needs liftgate service, which often carries an added fee (AEB Logistics states USD 100 for this).

Ask directly:

- Can they handle residential or non-commercial delivery addresses?
- Do they coordinate final-mile delivery to Amazon FBA facilities specifically?
- What’s their process if your receiving dock has restricted hours?

## Will You Receive Milestone Tracking and a Named Escalation Contact?

Real-time visibility means knowing exactly where your shipment sits at each checkpoint, not just “in transit.” Ask what milestones the forwarder tracks, from export customs and departure through import entry, arrival, and final delivery.

Ask whether tracking updates automatically or requires you to call for status. Sea freight tracking naturally updates less often than air, so ask how they handle communication gaps during ocean transit.

Just as important: who do you call when something goes wrong? A dedicated account manager who owns your shipment end-to-end is worth more than a support ticket queue during a customs hold. Confirm response-time expectations and whether escalation contact stays consistent across shipments, not rotating with each new booking.

## Documents to Gather Before Requesting China-USA Freight Quotes

Having the right paperwork ready before you contact a forwarder speeds up quoting and prevents costly misquotes. Missing details, especially on regulated or oversized cargo, are the most common cause of quote discrepancies later in the process.

### Which Cargo Details Affect a Hazardous or Regulated Freight Quote?

Gather your product’s HS code, UN number (if applicable), packing group, and Material Safety Data Sheet before requesting a quote. These details determine whether your cargo needs special carrier approval, extra documentation, or a hazmat surcharge.

Also have your cargo value, box count, total CBM (cubic meters), and gross weight ready. Forwarders use this to calculate chargeable weight accurately instead of estimating.

### Which Incoterm and Supplier Documents Should You Confirm First?

Confirm your Incoterm (FOB, EXW, DAP, or DDP) with your supplier before requesting quotes, since this determines who arranges origin pickup and who pays for it. Get your supplier’s factory address, cargo-ready date, and commercial invoice details lined up too.

Ask your supplier for a packing list and any export permits required for your product category. These documents move faster if they’re gathered before you contact a forwarder, not after.

### What U.S. Delivery-Site Details Prevent Final-Mile Surcharges?

Confirm whether your receiving site has a loading dock, since sites without one often need liftgate service at an added cost. Note delivery hours, any appointment-scheduling requirements, and whether the address is residential or commercial.

For Amazon FBA shipments, have your FBA booking form and shipment ID ready, since forwarders need these details to schedule delivery appointments through Amazon’s Carrier Appointment Request Portal.

## How to Compare Forwarder Answers Before You Book

The forwarder who answers your questions with specifics, not reassurance, is the one worth booking. Compare written quotes side by side, and treat vague language as a cost-risk signal rather than a communication quirk.

### What Does a Useful Written Quote Look Like?

A useful quote lists origin handling, freight cost, customs clearance, duties or a clear statement they’re separate, and final-mile delivery as individual line items. It also states the Incoterm the price is based on and any accessorial fees that could apply, like liftgate or storage.

Ask for a sample invoice from a past shipment similar to yours. If the forwarder can produce one without hesitation, that’s a good sign your quote will hold up once cargo moves.

### Which Vague Answers Signal Compliance or Cost Risk?

Watch for phrases like “we’ll handle it when it comes up” or “that’s usually not a problem” when you ask about dangerous-goods documentation or customs classification. These answers avoid commitment because the forwarder may not have direct experience with your commodity.

A forwarder confident in their process names the specific document, fee, or process step instead of generalizing. If you ask about UN classification and get a freight-rate answer instead, that’s a mismatch worth noting.

### How Should You Evaluate Transit Estimates and Cutoff Dates?

Ask for a realistic transit range instead of a single guaranteed number. Published general estimates for China-to-USA ocean freight door to door run around 30 to 40 days, while standard air freight runs about 8 to 10 days, and these can shift with customs processing, port congestion, and seasonal demand.

Ask about weekly cutoff schedules for booking cargo, since missing a cutoff pushes your shipment to the next available sailing or flight. A forwarder who explains cutoff timing clearly, instead of promising the fastest possible speed with no caveats, gives you a more reliable planning baseline.

### Can a China-to-USA freight forwarder ship hazardous goods and lithium batteries?

Some forwarders handle dangerous goods, but capability varies by UN number, packing group, and carrier acceptance on your specific lane. Ask for the forwarder’s direct experience with your exact classification, including whether they require a Dangerous Goods Declaration before booking, rather than a general yes-or-no answer.

### What should be included in an all-in DDP freight quote?

A complete DDP quote should include origin pickup, ocean or air freight, customs clearance, duties and taxes, and final-mile delivery to your U.S. address in one itemized total. Ask whether accessorial fees like liftgate service (often around USD 100) or storage past a free period are included or billed separately.

### How long does sea freight from China to the USA take?

Published general estimates put door-to-door ocean freight at approximately 30 to 40 days, though actual transit depends on port congestion, customs processing, and carrier schedules. Air freight runs faster, at roughly 8 to 10 days, for time-sensitive or higher-value cargo.

### Do I need a customs bond or importer of record for U.S. imports?

Yes, U.S. imports require a customs bond and an importer of record who remains responsible for duties and taxes. Amazon does not act as importer of record for FBA shipments and will not accept cargo arriving with unpaid duties, taxes, or shipping charges, so confirm this responsibility with your forwarder before your goods depart China.
